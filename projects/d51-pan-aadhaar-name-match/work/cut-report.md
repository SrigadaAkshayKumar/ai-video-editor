## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

One small mismatch between your P.A.N. card and your Aadhaar card can stall your first salary, and most freshers never check it until the problem has already started. If you are a final year student or a fresher about to join a tech company, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, you will learn the three-minute check to do this week, the two things that must match exactly, and the fix if they do not. Stay till the end because the third step is the one people skip. First, why this matters? On your joining day, the company will ask for your P.A.N. card, your Aadhaar card, your degree certificates, and your bank details. These are not random. Your P.A.N. is how the company deducts and reports your income tax. Your Aadhaar is used for your identity and for your provident fund account. Now think about what happens when your name or your date of birth is written differently on each one. The computer systems of the company, the bank, and the government cannot connect them, and when systems cannot connect, a human has to fix it, which takes days or weeks. Here is a fresher story. Meera joined on a Monday. Her Aadhaar said Meera K. Reddy, her P.A.N. said Meera Reddy Kondapali, and her degree said something else again. Her payroll details sat on hold for the first month. Nothing was wrong with Meera. The spelling was... Now the rule itself from the income tax department's own page, for new P.A.N. applicants, linking with Aadhaar is done automatically during the application. So if you applied for your P.A.N. recently, using your Aadhaar, you are most likely already linked. But for people who got their P.A.N. on or before the 1st of July 2017, linking is mandatory. The department's page says that if such a P.A.N. was not linked by the 30th of June 2023, it became inoperative. Why should that worry you? Because an inoperative P.A.N. is a P.A.N. that does not work, and in a job, your P.A.N. is the document your tax is built on. So the first check is simple. Do not assume. Look it up. Here is the three-minute check. It has three steps, and I will go in order. Step 1. Open your P.A.N. card and your Aadhaar card side by side. Compare your full name, letter by letter, and your date of birth, initials, species, and the order of surname and first name all count. Step 2. Go to the official income tax e-filing website, and use the link Aadhaar and view status option on the home page. Enter your P.A.N. and Aadhaar number and see what it says. Always type the address yourself. Never click a link sent on a message, because fake sites love this exact topic. Step 3. And this is the one people skip. Compare both of them with your degree certificate and your mark sheets. The company's background verification checks your education against the name in your documents. So a spelling that differs on your certificate can create the same delay later. So what if you find a mismatch? Do not panic and do not start before the joining date. Do it now while you are waiting. If the problem is in your P.A.N. details, apply for a correction through the official P.A.N. service. If the problem is in your Aadhaar, update it through the official Aadhaar update process. Pick the one that is wrong and fix that one so that everything matches your degree certificate. Keep a clean folder, both on your phone and in your email, which scans copies of your P.A.N. Aadhaar degree and mark sheets. And when the company's HR asks for documents, send the same names everywhere. If the name on any of them is different for a genuine reason, like a marriage or an old spelling, tell HR early in writing with a proof document, surprises are what delay joining. Early honesty does not. So to sum up, check your name and date of birth on your P.A.N. Aadhaar and degree. Check your P.A.N. link status on the official site and fix any mismatch before joining day, not after. Now tell me, have you ever had your name spelled differently on two documents?
