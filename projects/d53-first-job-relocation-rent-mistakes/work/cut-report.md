## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Most fresh shares lose money in the first week of their first job, and not because of salary. They lose it on rent. If you have an offer from a tech company in another city, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will tell you what to do before you book any room. The three renter mistakes that cost fresh shares thousands of rupees, and the one payment you should never make before your joining gate is confirmed. Stay till the end, because the third mistake is the one that traps most people. First, why this happens? When you get an offer in a new city, you are in a hurry. You have a joining date, you do not know the area, and you do not know anyone. That is exactly when people take bad decisions. And remember, your first salary does not arrive on day one. Usually, it comes at the end of your first month, or even later. So, in the first month, you are paying for rent, food and travel from your own pocket, or from your parents. So, the goal is simple. Spend as little as possible, commit to nothing long, and do not pay anyone you have not verified. Mistake number one, paying a deposit or advance before your joining date is confirmed. Joining dates can move. Many freshers have booked a flat or paid an advance, and then the company shifted the joining date by weeks. Some owners return the money, some do not. So, here is the rule, until you have a joining email with a clear date and location. Do not pay anything more than a small booking amount, and only when the owner gives a written receipt. And if anyone asks you to pay a fee to get the job itself, that is not rent. That is a scam, and it is different from everything in this video. Mistake number two, moving in with only a phone call and a promise. Whether it is a flat, a paying guest room, or a hostel, ask for these five things in writing. The monthly rent, the deposit amount, when and how the deposit is returned, the notice period if you want to leave, and what is included like water, electricity, food and internet. A message on a chat app is fine as a start, but a simple sign paper is better. Take a photo of the room on the day you enter with the date. If something is already broken, you do not want to pay for it when you leave. Also, never pay a large amount in cash without a receipt. Use bank or UPI payments, so there is a record. Now, the third mistake, the one that traps most people signing a long lease or a big deposit before you know where you will actually work. Here is a real situation that happens often. A fresher joins a company, takes a flat near the head office and signs for a year. Then, in the first weeks, the company says your project is in another office or you are asked to work from a different location. Now, the rent is stuck and the deposit is locked. So, for your first two months, choose something short and flexible, a paying guest room, a hostel or a shared flat with a short notice period. Stay with a friend or relative if you can. Only after your project and your work location are clear, look for a longer place. Also, check how long the journey will be. A cheap room that takes two hours a day on the road is not cheap. So, here is your plan, step by step. Step one, read your joining email carefully and note the city, the office and the date. Step two, book a short stay for the first two or three weeks. Use a verified place with reviews or a person you know. Step three, visit in person or ask a friend to visit before you pay. Do not pay for a room you have only seen in photos. Step four, after you join and your location is clear, search for a longer place and ask your new colleagues. They know the safe areas and fair prices and one more thing. Keep enough money for the first month because your salary will not come on day one. So, to sum up, do not pay before your joining is confirmed. Get the rent, deposit and exit terms in writing and do not sign a long lease until you know where you will work.
