You have a Letter of Intent. Maybe you even rejected another company for it. And now, after months of waiting, one question keeps you awake at night. What if they cancel it? Let's talk about it, honestly. What a Letter of Intent really is, how often offers actually get cancelled, the warning signs to watch for, and exactly what you can do to protect yourself. First, understand what you are holding. A Letter of Intent says, "The company intends to hire you. It shows you were selected, but it usually does not have a joining date, and it is not a full employment contract. The offer letter is the formal offer with your role, salary, and terms, and the joining letter or onboarding details tells you exactly when and where to report." So, the further you are along this chain, the more secure your position is, and LOI is the earliest and weakest of the three. Now, the real question, do companies actually cancel? Long delays are very common right now. Many candidates in 2026 are waiting months, even more than a year, between their Letter of Intent and their joining. Outride cancellations are much less common than delays, but they do happen. One example from the past. In 2023, LTI Mindtree asked a group of freshers who had been waiting since early 2022 to complete a new training program and pass tests. Reports said offers would be cancelled for those who did not take part. The IT employees union called it unethical. So, the honest picture is this, most LOI holders face delays, not cancellations. But, you cannot assume you are completely safe until you have a joining date. Here are warning signs that your offer may be at risk. 1. No communication at all for many months, while others from your batch and role are getting updates. 2. Your status on the candidate portal changes backwards, for example, from offered back to applied without any explanation. 3. The company asks you to complete new conditions like extra training or tests with strict deadlines. And 4. Wig answers when you ask about your timeline again and again. One sign alone does not mean cancellation. Portals often show strange statuses. But, if you see several of these together, take it seriously and start securing a backup. So, how do you protect yourself? First, keep a complete record, your letter of intent, every email, every portal screenshot, all with dates. Second, respond to every company request on time and in writing. If they ask for documents, training, or a test, never miss the deadline. Missing a deadline gives them an easy reason. Third, follow up politely through official channels, every few weeks, not every day. A short email with your candidate, ID, and a request for your expected timeline is enough. Fourth, keep applying elsewhere. A backup offer is not betrayal. It is common sense. And finally, if you believe you have been treated unfairly, IT. Employee groups like NITES have raised such cases with companies and with the labour ministry before. Keep your records ready if you choose to go that route. To sum up, a letter of intent shows intent, not a guarantee. Delays are common, cancellations are rarer, but they happen.
