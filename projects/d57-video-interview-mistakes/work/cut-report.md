## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your video interview can be rejected in the first thirty seconds and the reason is often not your answers. It is your camera, your background, or a tab you forgot to close. If you are a fresher with an online interview coming up, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will explain the three mistakes that quietly hurt freshers on video calls and the simple fix for each. Stay till the end because the third one has nothing to do with your technical skills. First, why this matters. In a video interview, the interviewer cannot see you walk in, shake hands, or read your body language the way they would in a room. So, they judge you through a small window on their screen. That window shows your face, your background, and how smoothly the call runs. If any of this is a problem, the interviewer is distracted before you say a single word. And a distracted interviewer remembers the problem, not your answer. The good news is that all three are in your control. Let us go one by one. Tip number one. A bad setup. Many freshers sit with a bright window behind them. So, their face becomes a dark shadow. Others place the laptop low so the camera looks up at their chin. Some sit on a bed with a messy room or a fan or a poster moving behind them. The fix is simple. Face the light. Do not sit with it behind you. Raise the laptop on a few books until the camera is at your eye level. Keep the background plain and tidy. A wall or a closed door is enough. And look at the camera while you speak, not only at the interviewer's face on the screen. That is how you create eye contact. Do this one test the day before. Record 30 seconds on your own laptop and watch it. You will fix three problems in two minutes. Mistake number two. Tech trouble that you could have predicted. Freshers often join the call at the exact minute on a weak connection with a half-charged laptop and the meeting software not yet installed. Then the link asks for an update, the microphone is muted and five minutes are gone. The interviewer's patience goes with them. Here is a short checklist. Install and test the meeting app the day before because different companies use different ones. Keep your laptop plugged in. Use headphones with a microphone so your voice is clear. Close every other tab and app. So notifications do not pop up. Keep your phone hotspot ready as a backup. And join five minutes early, not exactly on time. If the connection drops in the middle, do not panic. Rejoin quickly, say sorry once, and continue. Interviewers understand a drop. They do not forgive a candidate who has no plan. Now the third mistake, the one that surprises almost every fresher. Looking away from the screen to read something or to get help. In an online interview, your eyes give you away. If you keep glancing to one side, the interviewer wonders whether you are reading notes or whether someone is helping you off-camera. In technical rounds, some companies also use proctoring tools that can flag suspicious behavior and cheating can lead to rejection and sometimes a ban on future applications. You do not have to stay frozen. A small paper with your key points like your introduction and your project names is usually fine to glance at. But do not read answers word by word and never let anyone else help you or use another tool during the interview. If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask the interviewer politely at the start. When you are stuck, the honest line is bitter. Say give me a moment to think. That sounds confident, not weak. So here is what to do. Now, three steps. Step 1. Tonight, do a mock call with a friend. Check light, camera height and sound. Step 2. A day before every interview, test the meeting app, the charger and the internet and keep a backup. Step 3. Prepare short notes of key points, not full answers and look at the camera while you speak. Quick recap. Mistake 1. A bad setup. Mistake 2. Tech trouble you could have predicted. Mistake 3. Looking away for help. Now tell me in the comments which of these three have you been ignoring until today. Write the number 1, 2 or 3.
