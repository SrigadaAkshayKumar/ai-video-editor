## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Can a fresher negotiate salary? Most students never ask, because they are scared the offer will be taken back, and that fear costs some of them lakhs over the years. This video is for every fresher with an offer in hand or an interview coming up. By the end, you will know when you can negotiate, when you cannot, and the exact words to use so that nobody gets offended. First, the honest part, at many big hiring drives, the package is fixed for the whole batch. Everyone in your role gets the same offer letter with the same number. If that is your case, arguing will not change anything and it can only create a bad first impression. So, the first question is simple. Is your offer a batch offer or an individual offer? A batch offer usually comes from a campus drive or a mass hiring test with one package for one role. An individual offer comes after your own interviews, often from a smaller company or a product team where the manager decides the number. Only the second kind is really open for discussion. Keep that in mind because the next part depends on it. Here is the first mistake and it happens before the offer even arrives. Some freshers talk about salary in the very first round. Interviewers read that as a person who cares only about money and not about the work. The right time is after the company has said yes, at that point they have already decided they want you and you are not a risk anymore. If they ask your expected salary early, do not give a hard number say. I would like to understand the role fully first and I'm sure we can agree on something fair if we both feel it is a good match. The second mistake is comparing offers only by the big number. The C.T.C. Two offers can both say 6 lakh and still put very different money in your hand. One may include a large variable part that is paid only if targets are met. Another may include a joining bonus that you must return if you leave early. So, before you negotiate, ask for the full breakup in writing. Look for the fixed part, the variable part, the bonus conditions and the notice period. Sometimes the better ask is not a higher total. It can be a larger fixed part or a joining bonus without a harsh return clause. A smaller number that is certain can be better than a bigger number that is not. The third mistake is the one that hurts the most, inventing another offer. You say I have an offer from another company for more money. The recruiter replies can you share the letter and now you have two choices both bad. Hiring teams talk to each other and background verification can also show what you claimed. Being caught in a small lie can cost you the real offer. If you truly have another offer, you can mention it honestly. If you do not use something else, your skills, your project work, your internship or the market range for the role. All of these are real and none of them can be checked against you. So, what do you actually say? Here is a simple way in three steps. Step 1, thank them and show that you are excited about the role. Step 2, ask one clear question. For example, based on my skills and the market range for this role, is there any flexibility in the fixed pay? Step 3, stop talking and listen. Do not give a deadline, do not threaten to leave and whatever the answer is, thank them again. If the answer is no, you have lost nothing. If it is yes, one polite email just change your starting point and every future hike is calculated from that starting point. Let us recap. Check if your offer is a batch offer or an individual one. Wait until you have the offer in hand. Compare the full breakup, not just the headline number, never bluff and ask one polite question.
