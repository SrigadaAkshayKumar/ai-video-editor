## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Imagine this, you cleared the test, you cleared the interview, you have an offer letter, and then the company tells you, before you can join, you must complete a new training programme, and pass three more tests. This actually happened to hundreds of freshers at LTI Mindtree, back in 2023, and in 2026, with candidates reporting pre-joining training and knowledge test calls again. That old story is suddenly very relevant. In this video, I will tell you exactly what happened, why companies do this, and how to make sure you clear any extra test the first time. Here is what was reported in April 2023, a group of freshers had received offer letters from LTI Mindtree around January 2022. More than a year later, many of them were still waiting to join. Then the company introduced a programme called IGNITE. It was a training programme of about 6-7 weeks, with three assessment tests. To be considered for onboarding, candidates had to score 60% or more in all three tests, and even after passing, they would be onboarded in batches, based on business demand. Reports said around 600-700 freshers were affected, and one outlet reported that offers could be cancelled for those who did not take part. The IT employees union, NITS, publicly condemned the move, calling it unethical, because these candidates had already been selected once. So, why would a company test people it has already selected? From the company's side, there are three reasons. First time, if a candidate was selected more than a year ago, the company wants to check that their skills are still fresh before putting them on a client project. Second, filtering. When there are more selected candidates than open projects, a test helps the company decide who to onboard first. And third, readiness. A structured programme means new joiners arrive already trained on what the projects need, so they become billable faster. You may or may not agree with it. Many people feel it is unfair to test someone who was already selected. But understanding the reason helps you prepare for it. Now why am I talking about a 2023 story today? Because in 2026, similar patterns are showing up again, several companies run pre-joining training or online modules for selected candidates. And recently, cognizant candidates have reported getting calls from HR with technical questions, almost like a short knowledge test, before offer letters or joining dates. That is a candidate report, not an official policy, but it shows the direction. So the lesson is simple. Getting selected is no longer the finish line. In a slow hiring market, you may need to prove your skills again before you join. So here is how to make sure you clear any extra test the first time. One, revise the basics of what you were selected for, your programming language, data structures like arrays and strings, SQL and basic computer science, like OOP and DBMS. Two, take any pre-joining modules seriously, finish them early, take notes and practise the quizzes. Companies often build their tests from these same modules. Three, practise timed tests. Many assessments are online and timed, so speed matters as much as knowledge. And four, keep your project ready to explain. If a call or test asks about your work, a clear two-minute explanation makes a strong impression. And one more thing, if a programme comes with a deadline, respond on time in writing. Missing a deadline is the easiest way to lose an offer you already earned. To sum up, in 2023, LTI mind tree made waiting freshers passed a new training programme before joining and the union called it unfair. In 2026, similar pre-joining checks are appearing again, so treat selection as halfway and keep your skills sharp until your first day.
