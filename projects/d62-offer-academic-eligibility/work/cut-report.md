Your offer letter can be cancelled with one line, and the line says, "You do not meet our academic eligibility criteria." If you are a final year student, or a fresher waiting to join, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will show you the three academic things that decide whether your offer survives the exact check to do this week, and what to do if the mail ever arrives. Stay till the end, because the third thing is the one more student's never check. First, this is not a rare story. In October 2022, news outlets reported that Vipro, Infosys, and Tech Mahindra had cancelled offers of some freshers who were waiting to join. Candidates said the mail read, "It has been identified that you are not meeting our academic eligibility criteria, hence your offer stands null and void." Many of them had waited three to four months for a joining date before the mail came. Reports differ on how many people were affected, so I will not quote a number. These were reports based on candidate mails, and the companies had not commented when they were published. What I want you to notice is the reason it was not about skill or interview performance, it was about paper marks, degree status, and documents. So let us make sure your paper is safe. Thing number one, your marks, most companies set a minimum percentage or CGPA in your 10th, 12th, and degree. The exact cut-off differs from company to company, and it is written in the campus or hiring notice you applied through. Do not trust a friend's memory, open that notice again. Here is the trap, some students round up your CGPA is 7.45 and you write 7.5 on the form, or you convert it to a percentage with a formula that your university does not use. Background verification compares the form with your mark sheet line by line, and a small round up becomes a mismatch. So write your marks exactly as the mark sheet says, if the form asks for percentage, use your university's official conversion and keep a copy of it. Thing number two, backlogs, many companies ask for no active backlogs at the time of joining, and some ask for none at all in your degree. This is a company rule, not a law, so you must read it for your own offer. The mistake is this, a student has one pending paper, thinks it is small, and says nothing. Or the student says there is no backlog because the exam is already written, but the result is not out. When the result day comes and the paper is cleared, all is well. When it is not, the company can treat it as not meeting the criteria. So make a simple list today, which papers are pending, when is the next exam, and what does your offer say about backlogs. If a paper is pending, do not hide it and do not panic. Prepare for that exam first, before any new course or certificate. Now the third thing, the one I promised your final degree documents, your offer is usually given while you are still studying. So the company accepts you on trust and asks for the final proof letter. That proof can be your final semester mark sheet, your provisional certificate, or your degree certificate, which one and by which date is written in your offer letter or onboarding mail. If you miss that date, the company can delay your joining or even withdraw the offer. And this is where students lose, because the university takes weeks to issue documents and nobody checks until the mail arrives. So do three things, find the document deadline in your offer, ask your college office today when the provisional certificate will be ready, and if there will be a delay, write to the company early with the reason and your college's date. A polite early mail is far better than a late excuse. What if the cancellation mail does arrive? Stay calm and do it in order. Step 1, read the mail twice and find the exact reason it gives. Step 2, reply politely in writing and ask which document or criteria it refers to. Attach your mark sheets. Step 3, if the reason is a document delay, ask for a new date. If you think the company is wrong, ask your college placement cell to write to them as well. Do not send angry mails and do not threaten. Keep every mail in one folder. I am keeping this general because every company has its own process and your offer letter is the final guide. So here is what to do this week. Open your offer and hiring notice and write down the marks rule, the backlog rule and the document deadline. Match every mark you filled in with your mark sheet. Make a list of pending papers and ask your college office for the date of your provisional certificate. Quick recap, one exact marks, two no hidden backlogs, three documents before the deadline.
