If you graduated in 2024 or 2025, and you still don't have a job, you have probably asked yourself this, "Am I still a fresher, or is it already too late?" You are not alone. The last two years have been tough for it, hiring, and a lot of good students are in exactly this position. In this video, I will explain how batch eligibility really works, which kinds of opportunities are still open to you, how to explain your gap in interviews, and what to do this month. First, understand how it works. Every fresher drive has an eligibility line. It usually says which graduation years can apply, along with conditions like minimum percentage, and no pending backlogs. There is no single rule across the industry. Some drives are only for the current final year batch, others accept the last one or two batches, and some programs are open to anyone who meets the education criteria. So, the answer to "Am I too old?" is not yes or no. It is read the eligibility line of each drive. Many students give up without even checking. So, what is still open to a 2024 or 2025 graduate? One, off-campus drives that accept previous batches. Check every big company's career space because the batch list changes with every drive to mid-sized companies and product companies. Many of them hire based on skills and projects, not graduation year. Three, internships and apprenticeships. Some are open to recent graduates, and an internship that converts to a full-time job is a very real path. And four, contract and service desk roles. They may not be your dream job, but they give you real work experience. After one year, you are no longer competing as a fresher. You are an experienced candidate, which opens a different set of jobs. The question every interviewer will ask is, "What have you been doing since you graduated?" Never say, "Nothing. I was just preparing." Instead, show what you built and learned. For example, since graduating, I've been preparing for placements, and during that time, I completed a certification in SQL and built a data dashboard project that analyzes sales data, which is on my GitHub. If you had a personal reason, like health or family responsibilities, it is fine to say it briefly and honestly. Then move straight to what you learned. Do this every month and track your applications. Consistency beats luck. To sum up, your batch year is not a fixed barrier. Every drive has its own eligibility line.
