## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

You finished your training. You cleared the assessments. Your friends are getting projects one by one. And you are still waiting, with no project, and no idea what happens next. In I.T. companies, this is called being on the bench. I.T. is very common, especially for freshers right now. But how you use your time on the bench can decide how fast you get a good project, and how your first year goes. In this video, I will explain what the bench is, why freshers end up there, what usually happens, the risks, and exactly how to get out fast. The bench simply means you are an employee, you are getting paid, but you are not assigned to any client project right now. I.T. services companies earn money when their employees work on client projects, which is called being billable. When you are on the bench, you are not billable. So, for the company, the bench is a cost, and they want to move people from the bench to projects as quickly as possible. That is actually good news for you, the company, and you want the same thing, you getting a project. Why do freshers end up on the bench? Mostly for reasons outside your control. One, the project that your batch was hired for got delayed or cancelled. Two, there are not enough new projects that match your training technology at that moment. Three, many projects want experienced people first, so freshers wait longer, and sometimes there is one reason inside your control. Low scores in training assessments or a week project interview can push you further down the list. So, what usually happens while you are on the bench, you will typically be asked to stay available and to keep learning. Many companies have an internal job portal or talent marketplace where open project roles are posted and where you can apply or be picked. Project managers may call you for a short project interview where they check if your skills match their project. If it goes well, you get allocated and the bench period ends. Every company has its own bench policy including attendance expectations and what happens after a long bench period. So read your company's HR policy portal and know your own company's rules. Now the risks, a few weeks on the bench is normal. A very long bench is where problems start. First, your skills can get rusty and your confidence drops. Second, you may end up accepting a project that does not match your interests like support work just to get off the bench. And third, in tough times, companies review employees who have been on the bench for a long time. So the bench is not somewhere you want to stay quietly. So here is how to get out of the bench fast one. Check the internal job portal every day and apply to every role that reasonably matches your skills. Don't wait to be picked. Two, use the time to get one internal certification or course in a skill that is in demand like cloud data or AI bench. Time is the best learning time you will ever get because you are paid to learn. Three, prepare for project interviews like real interviews, your training technology, your project and your basics. Four, talk to people, your trainers, your seniors and your resource manager. Many project roles are filled through recommendations. Let them know what you are good at and that you are available. And five, be flexible but not desperate, accept a reasonable project even if it is not perfect and grow from there. You can move later. To sum up, the bench means you are paid but not on a project. I.T. is common for freshers and usually not your fault, but a long bench has real risks. So check the portal daily, learn a skill, prepare for interviews, network and stay flexible. If you are on the bench right now, comment how long you have been there and your technology. Others in the same situation can share what worked for them.
