## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your offer letter says six lakh rupees a year. So, why will your first salary credit be much less than 50,000 rupees a month? This video is for every fresher who has an offer letter or is about to get one. By the end, you will know exactly where the money goes, what changed this September that can cut your take-home even more, and one check you must do before you sign. Stay for the third deduction because that is the one almost nobody explains. First, the word on every offer letter, CTC, it stands for Cost to Company. CTC is not what you receive. It is what the company spends on you in a year. That includes your salary, the company's share of your provident fund insurance, and sometimes a variable pay that is paid only if targets are met. So, when you see six lakh rupees, do not divide by 12 and plan your rent. Divide by 12 only to get the company's monthly cost. Your bank account will see a smaller number. Let us take a simple example. Remember, this is only an example. Every company structures salary differently. Say your monthly CTC is 50,000 rupees. Your basic salary is half of it, 25,000 rupees. Now, look at the three things that come out. Deduction 1. The company's own provident fund share. This is 12% of your wages and the company counts it inside your CTC, but it never reaches your bank. If it is calculated on 25,000, that is 3,000 rupees a month. Deduction 2. Your own provident fund share another 12%, so another 3,000 rupees cut from your salary every month. This money is yours and it sits in your provident fund account, but you cannot spend it now. Deduction 3. The one almost nobody explains. Professional tax. A small state tax on salaried people. In many states, it is only about 200 rupees a month, but it depends on your state, so check yours. Add it up and your in-hand salary lands around 43,000 to 44,000 rupees and that is before any insurance or variable pay adjustment. That is roughly 12% less than the number you were dreaming about. Now, the update that matters this month. On the seventeenth of September, 2026, the government raised the wage ceiling for mandatory provident fund membership from 15,000 rupees to 25,000 rupees a month. This was reported by Mint and Money Control. What does it mean for you? If your provident fund was earlier calculated on only 15,000 rupees, it was 1,800 rupees a month from you. Under the new ceiling, it can become 3,000 rupees a month. That is 1,200 rupees more deducted or 14,400 rupees less in your bank every year. But there is a catch, it depends on how your company structures your salary. If your provident fund was already calculated on your full basic pay above 25,000 rupees, you may see no change at all. So, do not panic, check your salary slip. Now, the good news, income tax. Under the new tax regime, a salaried person gets a standard deduction of 75,000 rupees and income up to 12,000 rupees gets a rebate under section 87A. That was reported by WION News in simple words, with a fresher package of around 6,000 rupees, you will most likely pay no income tax at all this year. If your company deducts tax anyway, ask the payroll team which regime they used because you can choose a regime in the company portal. Also remember, the provident fund money is not lost. It earns interest and it is yours when you leave the job. So, here is your checklist before you sign anything. 1. Ask for the salary breakup. Basic allowance is provident fund variable pay, insurance. If the offer only gives one number, ask for the breakup in writing. 2. Find out how much is fixed and how much is variable. Variable pay is not guaranteed. 3. Ask if provident fund is calculated on the full basic or on the ceiling. After September, this changes your take home. 4. Look for bond notice period and joining bonus clawback clauses. Money you owe back is also part of your real salary. 5. After your first salary, compare your slip with the breakup line by line. Mistakes are easier to fix in the first month. To sum up, CTC is what the company spends. In hand is what you receive. Two provident fund shares and a small state tax explain most of the gap and the new September ceiling can widen it.
