There is a payment in your salary structure that you will never see on a single pay slip, and it can be worth lakhs. Most freshers lose it without even knowing it existed, simply because they quit a few months too early. It is called Graduity. If you are a student or a fresher heading into your first tech job, stay till the end. You will learn exactly who gets it, how it is calculated, and the one date you should mark on your calendar before you ever write a resignation mail. First, what is Gratuity? It is a lump sum that your employer pays you as a reward for long service. It is not paid every month. It is paid once when you leave the company whether you resign, retire, or if something happens to you, then it goes to your nominee. Think of it as a loyalty bonus that the law forces the company to give. Today, Gratuity falls under the Code on Social Security, 2020, which replaced the old payment of Gratuity Act of 1972. Now, here is the catch. It does not apply to every company. In general, it covers establishments with 10 or more employees. For most tech companies that hire freshers, that is not a problem, but if you join a very small startup, ask whether Gratuity applies. Now, the part everyone wants, how much do you get? The formula is simple. Take your last drawn monthly wages. Multiply by 15 divided by 26. Then, multiply by the number of years you worked. Why? 15 and 26. The law says you get 15 days of wages for every year of service and a month is counted as 26 working days. But look at the word wages. It does not mean your full salary. It mainly means your basic pay plus DNS allowance, not your bonus, not your special allowance, not your CTC. Here is an example. Say your basic pay is 40,000 rupees a month and you work for 5 years. 40,000 times 15 divided by 26 times 5. That comes to about 1,15,000 rupees. So, your basic pay is the number that matters. That is why a salary structure with a higher basic gives a bigger Gratuity. Now, the date I promised. The law generally asks for 5 years of continuous service with the same employer before you get Gratuity on resignation. That means if you join work for 3 years and switch, you usually get zero Gratuity from that company. Zero. Not a smaller amount. Nothing. There are exceptions. If the employee dies or becomes disabled, the 5-year condition does not apply. And there is one newer point you should know. Service higher directly on a written fixed term contract can qualify after just one year with the amount worked out in proportion to the time served. So, when you get your offer, check if it is a permanent role or a fixed term contract. Also years are counted by continuous service. So long unpaid breaks or a rejoining can complicate things. Do not guess. Ask HR in writing how your service will be counted. So, what should you do about this as a fresher? One. Read your offer letter and appointment letter for the word Gratuity. Many letters mention it in the benefits or CTC section. Two. Do not make job decisions only on a bigger CTC number. A company that pays a higher basic and includes Gratuity can be worth more over the years than one that pushes everything into special allowance. Three. Before you resign, count your completed years. If you are close to 5 years, even a few months can change what you take home. Four. When you do leave, ask for your Gratuity calculation in writing and check that it uses your last basic pay and dearness allowance. Some companies pay more than the legal minimum. So, also read your own company policy. And remember a part of a great duty payout can be taxable depending on the rules that apply to you. So, do not spend it before you know the tax. To sum up, Gratuity is a lump sum paid when you leave, worked out from your basic pay 15 days for every year of service and you usually need 5 years with the same employer to get it.
