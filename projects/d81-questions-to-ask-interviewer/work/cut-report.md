## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

The interview is going well, then the interviewer smiles and says, "Do you have any questions for us?" and you say, "No, sir, all clear." That one sentence can quietly cost you the offer. If you are a fresher preparing for interviews in the next five minutes, I will show you why this last question matters. The three questions that make you look serious, and the questions that make the interviewers lose interest, stay till the end, because the third mistake is one almost every fresher makes. First, why do interviewers ask this at all? It is not a formality. By this point, they already know your marks and your answers. Now they want to see one more thing. Are you curious about the work, or do you just want any job? Think about it from their side. They interview many freshers in one day, most say no questions. So the few who ask something sensible are remembered. Not because the question is clever, but because it shows you have thought about the role. Here is the catch. A bad question hurts as much as no question. So let us fix that. Question number one. What will a fresher in this team work on in the first three months? This shows you are thinking about the actual job. Not only the salary, the answer also tells you something useful. It may be training first, then a small module, then real tasks with a senior. Now you know what to prepare for. You can also ask, what does a good first six months look like for someone in this role? It is the same idea. You are telling them you want to do well, and asking what that means. Keep it short. One question, then listen to the full answer. Do not interrupt. Question number two. How does the team help new joiners learn? Is there training, a buddy or a senior who reviews your work? Why does this work? Because companies want people who will grow. And this question says, you plan to grow. It is also a question for you. If nobody guides new joiners, that is something you should know before you accept. A tip. Listen for words like mentor, code review or training plan. If the interviewer gives a clear answer, great. If they hesitate, do not react. Just say thank you and note it in your mind. Stay polite. You are not testing them. You are showing interest. Question number three. The simplest and the most useful. What are the next steps in the process and when can I expect to hear from you? This one is practical. It tells you whether to expect another round, a document check or an offer mail. And it shows you are organised. You also stop refreshing your inbox every hour because now you know the timeline. Write down what they say if they say within a week and nothing comes in eight days. You can send one polite follow up mail. That is perfectly fine. Three questions. The work, the learning and the next step. Remember these three. Now the questions to avoid. The third mistake I promised. First do not ask about salary. Leave or work from home in a first round. There is a time for that after you have an offer. Asking too early sounds like you care only about benefits. Second do not ask something the company website already answers. Like what does your company do? That shows you did no homework. And the third mistake the one almost every fresher makes. Asking a question you already know just to fill the silence. Like how is the culture here? It sounds copied because it is. Interviewers hear it all day. Ask something about the real work instead. A good test. Before you ask, think, would I really want to know this answer? If yes, ask. If no, skip it. So to sum up, when they ask, if you have questions, never say no. Ask about the work, ask about learning and ask about the next step. Avoid salary, avoid website questions and avoid copied lines.
