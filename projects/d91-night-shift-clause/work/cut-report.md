## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your offer letter says Software Engineer. But hidden in one line is a clause that can put you on night shift from your very first week. This video is for every fresher who has an offer or has just joined. By the end, you will know the three shift questions to ask before day one and the one thing you must get in writing because a promise on a phone call is worth nothing. First, why does this happen at all? Many projects serve clients in the United States, the United Kingdom or Australia. When the client wakes up, your day may be ending. So, the team has to be online during the client's working hours and for some projects that means evening or night in India. Support and operations projects often run around the clock in rotating shifts. Development projects can also have a late overlap where you work a few hours with the client team every day. And here is the catch. Most offer letters do not name your shift. They only say that the company can assign you working hours based on business needs. That one line is the clause I mean. Question 1. Which shift will I work and is it fixed or rotating? Ask your recruiter or the onboarding team in a polite email. A fixed day shift, a fixed night shift and a rotating shift are three very different lives. Rotating means your sleep time changes every week or every month and that is tiring in the first year when you are also learning the job. Also ask how long the shift lasts. Some projects are night shift only for the first few months during training or knowledge transfer. Others are permanent. Do not worry about sounding negative. Asking about working hours is normal and no company cancels an offer for a polite question. Question 2. What do I get for working at odd hours? Some companies pay a shift allowance for night or late shifts. Others include it inside the package and some pay nothing extra at all. There is no single rule for every company. So you must ask is there a shift allowance? How much and is it part of my fixed pay or paid only on the days I work that shift? Then ask about travel. Many companies give a cab for late hours, especially for night shifts. Ask whether the cab is provided, who pays and what happens if you are on a late shift and there is no cab. The trap here is a verbal answer. If the recruiter says you will get an allowance, ask them to confirm it by email. If it is not written, it may not exist when your first salary comes. Question 3. What is the way out if the shift does not suit me? Your health matters. Late shifts affect sleep, food and study time. Ask whether you can request a shift change after your training or after a few months and who decides, your manager or the human resources team. If you have a genuine reason, like a medical condition or a safety concern, tell human resources in writing early. Do not wait until you are exhausted. Companies are far more helpful when you raise it politely and with the reason than when you simply stop turning up. Here is a small story. Imagine a fresher Ravi who joined, found out in week 2 that his project was a night shift and said nothing for 3 months. His health dropped, his learning dropped and his first appraisal suffered. If Ravi had asked early, his manager might have moved him to an overlap shift. So what do you do this week? If you already have an offer, write one short email and ask the 3 questions. Keep the reply if you have already joined. Check your offer letter for the working hours line and ask your manager about the shift plan for the next 3 months. And during any late shift, protect yourself, sleep at fixed hours, keep your cab details saved and tell a family member or friend your shift timings. The one thing to remember, shifts are not a punishment and not a secret. They are a project requirement and you have every right to understand them before you say yes. Quick recap. Ask which shift and whether it is fixed. Ask about allowance and cab and get it in writing. And know how to ask for a change early and politely. Now tell me in the comments. Has your company told you your shift day night or rotating? Write your shift type and one thing you wish you had known before joining.
