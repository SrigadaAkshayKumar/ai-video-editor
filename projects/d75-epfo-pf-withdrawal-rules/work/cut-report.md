The PF Board has just changed how long you wait to take your whole Provident Fund out after you lose or leave a job, and if you are a fresher about to join your first company, one wrong move with this money can cost you luck later. In the next five minutes, I will explain what changed on the 19th of November, what has not changed, and the one mistake freshers make with their PF when they switch jobs. Stay till the end, because the third point is the one most people learn too late. First, what happened? On the 19th of November, the central board of the EPFO, The Employees, Provident Fund Organisation, met in its 240th meeting, chaired by the Union Labour Minister. They approved three big changes. One, the waiting period for a full final withdrawal after unemployment is cut from 12 months to 2 months. Two, the minimum balance you must leave in your account during a partial withdrawal is cut from 25% to 10%. So, you could access up to 90% of an eligible balance instead of 75%. Three, retirees get an optional plan to take their money in monthly, quarterly, or half-yearly installments. Now, they catch several reports including PTI and scroll. Say, these changes are not enforced yet. The Labour Ministry still has to notify them. So, treat this as approved, not life. Let me make the numbers simple. Say, your eligible PF balance is 10 lakh rupees. Under the old minimum balance rule, you had to leave 2.5 lakh. So, you could take up to 7.5 lakh. Under the new rule, you would leave 1 lakh and could take up to 9 lakh. Sounds great, right? But here is what has not changed. The pension part, the money in the employee's pension scheme, still has a 36-month waiting period for withdrawal. And a partial withdrawal still needs at least 12 months of service. A fresher in the first year cannot use it. Now, the part that matters for you, when freshers leave their first job, many are tempted to withdraw the whole PF just because it is now easier. Please do not do this by habit. Here is why. First, your UN number stays with you for life. When you join a new company, you give the same UN and you can transfer your old PF balance to the new account online. Your money stays in one place and keeps earning interest. Second, every rupee you take out stops compounding. A small balance taken out at 23 can be a big loss at 58. Third, and this is the trap. Withdrawing early can also mean tax being deducted on it. So, before you claim, check your service period and ask your payroll team. So, here is what to do this week. One login to the member portal and check that your UAN is active and your mobile number and ADHAR are linked. Two, when you switch jobs, transfer do not withdraw unless you truly need the cash. Three, check the notification from the labour ministry before you plan around the new rules. Rules approved in a meeting are not rules you can claim yet. Companies and PF offices follow the notified rule not the news headline. To sum up, the waiting time for a full PF withdrawal after unemployment may fall to two months. The minimum balance may fall to 10% but nothing is live until it is notified and your best move as a fresher is still to keep your PF growing.
