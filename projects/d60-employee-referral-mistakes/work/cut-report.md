## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Most freshers ask for a referral in the worst possible way, and that is why the answer is almost always silence. If you are a fresher applying off campus or a final year student who wants a job in a tech company, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will show you the three things that make an employee actually refer you and a short message you can copy. Stay till the end because the second mistake is the one that gets you ignored on the first message. First, why a referral matters at all? When you apply on a careers page, your resume sits in a very big pile. When an employee refers you, a person inside the company puts your name forward, and recruiters often look at those resumes first. But here is the part nobody tells you. A referral does not mean a job, it only means someone looks at your resume. You still have to clear the tests and the interviews. And for the employee, a referral puts their own name on the line, so they refer only people they trust. Your job is to make that decision easy for them. Mistake number one, sending the same message to fifty strangers. You open a company page, you find fifty employees, and you copy paste the same message to all of them. Hi sir, please refer me. Most people will not even open it. Instead, send fewer messages, but make each one personal. Pick people who studied at your college or who work in the team you want. Read their profile for one minute. Then write one line that shows you did. For example, hi, I saw that you also studied at our college. I am applying for the graduate engineer role, and I have a question about the team. Would you have two minutes this week? Notice something. You are not asking for a referral yet. You are asking for a small conversation. That is much easier to say yes to. Mistake number two, the one I promised. Asking for the referral before you have shown anything. A stranger cannot refer you if they know nothing about you. So make it easy. Send a message with three things only. The exact role you are applying for. Two lines about your skills and your best project. And your resume as a single file. Do not write a long story about your family or your problems. Do not send ten different resumes. And never say, please do it. I am desperate. Calm and clear always works better than desperate. Also check the role first. If the job needs two years of experience and you are a fresher, no referral will fix that. Pick roles that are really open for freshers. Mistake number three, disappearing after the referral or never saying thank you. If someone refers you, thank them the same day. Tell them if you clear a round. Tell them if you do not. People remember who was polite and they will help you again or help your friend. And if nobody replies, do not take it personally. Employees are busy. Wait one week, send one short follow up and then move on. Never send a third message. One more thing. Never pay anyone for a referral. If a person or a group asks you for money to get you a job, that is a scam. A real referral is free. So here is what to do from today. Pick five people, not fifty. Read their profile for one minute. Send a short personal message that asks a small question. When someone replies, share the role, two lines about you and your resume. And always say thank you. Quick recap. Mistake one, copy paste messages. Mistake two, asking before showing anything. Mistake three, no thanks and no follow up. Now tell me in the comments.
