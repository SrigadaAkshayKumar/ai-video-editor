## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your offer letter says, Variable Pay, Joining Bonus, and Retention Bonus. But in some companies, that money is not yours until a condition is met. And one wrong move can mean paying it back. This video is for every fresher with an offer letter that has a package bigger than the monthly salary. In the next 5 minutes, I will show you 3 words in your offer that decide whether you really get that money. Stay till the end because the third one is the one nobody reads. First, the basics. Your package has 2 kinds of money. Fixed Pay is paid every month, whatever happens. Variable Pay is a target amount. It is paid only if the company, your team, or you meet a target. The offer letter shows the full amount. But the amount you actually receive can be lower, and sometimes zero. Here is a real example. In October this year, News Report said TCS told senior employees from grade C3A and above that they would get no quarterly variable pay because the company missed its internal targets. Junior employees were told they would get their full eligible pay. This was the company's own memo, reported by Reuters and carried by Mint and The Economic Times. Now notice this was about senior staff. But the lesson is for you, variable pay is a target, not a promise. So when you compare 2 offers, compare the fixed pay first. Word number 1, payout. Look for how the variable pay is paid. Is it monthly, quarterly, or yearly? Is it paid at 100% or does it depend on a rating? Is it linked to company performance or only to your own? Why does this matter? Because a fresher who counts the full variable amount in the monthly budget and plans rent and a loan against it can be in trouble in a bad quarter. So do this today. Take your offer letter and write two numbers on a paper. The money that comes every month for sure and the money that may come. Plan your life on the first number only. Word number 2, bonus. A joining bonus is paid when you join or soon after. A retention bonus is paid after you stay for some time. Many offers say that if you leave before a fixed period, say 12 months, you must return the bonus fully or partly. Remember Rabi, a fresher, he took a joining bonus, then got a better offer after 7 months. The company asked for the bonus back and it was deducted from his last salary. He had never read that line. The details differ from company to company so I will not give you one rule. What you do is this, find the sentence with the words return, refund or recover. Check the period, the amount and whether it reduces over time. Word number 3, the one nobody reads, recovery. Some agreements say the company can recover dues from your final salary. That can include a bonus, training cost or notice period shortfall. So if you leave, your last settlement can come very low. Do these three things. One, read the clause now before you sign not on your last day. Two, if anything is unclear, ask HR to explain it in writing by mail and keep that mail. Three, before you resign, calculate what you will owe and compare it with your new offer. And one more thing, never sign a blank or a changed page without reading it. If a number differs from your offer letter, ask first. Quick recap, variable pay is a target not a promise. Bonus often comes with a return clause and recovery can come out of your last salary.
