Your last salary can be held back after you resign, and most freshers only find out when the money does not come. If you are a fresher who plans to leave your first job one day, or you simply want to know your rights, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will explain what full and final settlement means, the three things to check before you sign anything, and the rule that says how fast you must be paid. Stay till the end, because the third thing is the one that decides whether your next employer trusts you. First, what is full and final settlement? People call it FNF. When you leave a company, the company does a calculation, it adds up everything it owes you, and subtracts everything you owe it. The result is your final payment. What can be on the add side? Your salary for the days you worked in the last month, leave in cashment if your company pays for unused leave. Any bonus or incentive that was already due, and approved expense claims. What can be on the subtract side? Notice period shortfall. If you left earlier than your notice period, any joining bonus or training cost you have to repay under your agreement, tax that was not yet deducted, and any company dues, like an unreturned laptop or access card. So, it is not one simple payment, it is a statement with many lines, and you have every right to read each line. Now, the rule I promised, the code on wages came into force on the 21st of November 2025. It says that when an employee resigns, the wages payable must be paid within two working days. That is a big change because the older rule covered only people who earned below a small monthly limit, and mainly when the employer ended the job. The new code has no such salary limit, and it clearly includes resignation. But here is the catch. Two working days applies to wages, which means your salary and allowances. It does not automatically cover everything in the statement. Gratuity, for example, has its own timeline of thirty days. And news reports this month say many companies are still struggling to fully put the new labour codes into practice. So, do not assume the money will arrive in two days. Also, do not accept that it takes sixty days without asking. Ask your HR politely in writing for the date on which each part will be paid. Now, the three things to check. Number one, ask for the statement in writing with every line explained. If you see a deduction, you do not understand ask what it is and which clause of your agreement allows it. A short polite mail is enough. Number two, do not sign a no-duce declaration before you have seen the numbers. Some people sign a paper that says nothing is pending and only later notice a missing amount. Check first, then sign. Number three, the one I promised, collect your documents on the last day. Your relieving letter, your experience letter, your last salary slips, and your provident fund details, which means your UN stays the same and you apply to transfer the old balance instead of withdrawing it. Why does the third one decide trust? Because your next employer's background verification will ask for exactly these papers. A missing relieving letter can delay your new offer, even when the settlement money was perfectly fine. What if the payment is late or something looks wrong? Stay calm and follow the order. Step one, send a polite mail to HR and your manager with the date you left and the amount you expect. Step two, if there is no answer in a week, send one reminder and mention the two working day rule for wages. Step three, if it still does not move, you can approach the labour authority in your area or take advice from a labour lawyer. Keep your appointment letter, your pay slips, and your mails in one folder. Never send angry messages and never threaten. Clear dates and clear numbers work better than anger. I am keeping this part general because rules and processes can differ by state and by company. Your appointment letter is the final guide for your case. So, here is what to do from today. Keep a folder with your offer letter, appointment letter, and every salary slip. When the day comes, ask for the statement in writing, check every line, and sign no dues only after that. And collect your relieving letter and experience letter before you walk out. Quick recap. One, a written statement. Two, no signing before checking. Three, collect your documents.
