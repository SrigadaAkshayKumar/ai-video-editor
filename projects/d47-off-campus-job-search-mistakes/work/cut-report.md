## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Sending a hundred applications and getting zero replies feels like the market is closed. It usually is not. Most freshers are rejected off-campus for three small mistakes that they never get to hear about. If you were not placed in your campus drive or you are applying outside campus, this video is for you. By the end, you will know how to apply who to message and what to fix first. And the third mistake is the one that quietly wastes the most applications. First, understand how off-campus hiring works because it is not the same as campus. On campus, the company comes to you and the college gives you the list. Off-campus, nobody gives you anything. You find the opening, you apply, and a recruiter decides in a few seconds whether to open your profile. Most companies get far more applications than they can read. So many are filtered by a simple check before any human looks. Does your degree match the opening? Does your graduation year match? Do the skills in the post appear in your profile? If not, you are filtered out even if you are good. That is why sending the same profile everywhere does not work. Mistake number one. Applying to everything. A fresher sees fifty openings and applies to all of them, including roles that ask for three years of experience or a different skill. That feels productive, but it is noise. Instead, make a small list of roles that clearly say fresher, graduate trainee, associate or entry level and where your degree and year are allowed. Read the post fully then apply to ten of those. Not a hundred of the wrong kind and always apply on the company's own careers page when you can. A job post that only says send money or send your documents on a chat app is not a job. Never pay anyone for an interview or a job. Mistake number two. A profile that proves nothing. Think like the recruiter, they see the line, good at python and java. Everyone writes that what they want is proof. So, under every skill, show one small thing you built. A simple project with a link, a few lines on what it does and what you learned. Even a small project that works is stronger than ten skills with no proof. Keep your name and your email the same everywhere. Use a professional email address and make sure your profile links open without a login. A recruiter who gets an error will not try twice. Now the third mistake, the one that wastes the most effort. Applying and then waiting. On campus, the college is your bridge. Off campus, you must build your own and the easiest bridge is a short polite message to a real person. Find recruiters and engineers from the company on a professional networking site. Send a short note. Say who you are, which opening you applied to and one line on why you fit. Do not write a long story and never ask for a job in the first message. Ask one clear question or ask them to look at your application. Many freshers also forget referrals. Tell friends, seniors and relatives that you are looking. A referral does not guarantee a job, but it often gets your profile opened by a human. So what do you do this week? Day one, fix your profile. One page resume project links that open the same name everywhere. Day two, make your list of ten roles that truly fit a fresher. Day three, apply on the careers pages and send a short message to one real person for each application. Then repeat every week. Keep a simple sheet with the company the date and what happened. Follow up once after a week politely. Most freshers stop too early and the offer often goes to the person who kept going. To sum up, do not apply to everything. Show proof for every skill and do not wait alone because a short message to one real person beats a hundred silent applications.
