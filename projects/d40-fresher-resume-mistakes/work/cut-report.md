## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

A recruiter opens your resume and decides in a few seconds, and most fresher resumes lose that decision for the same four reasons. If you are applying for your first job, stay with me. By the end of this video, you will know the four resume mistakes that quietly get you rejected and the one mistake that can get you in trouble even after you get the offer. First, a simple test. Recruiters and hiring experts often suggest this one. Cover your name at the top of your resume and read it. If it could belong to any other student in your batch, you have a problem, because the person reading it does not know you your college or your project. They only know what is written on the page. A former Google recruiter Farah Sharghi told CNBC that the biggest mistake is writing for a person who already knows you, which is yourself. Every mistake in this video comes from that one idea. Mistake #1, Adjectives instead of proof. Excellent communication. Team player. Hard-working. Quick learner. Every fresher writes these, so they carry no weight. The same recruiter says if you cannot prove a skill with a specific example, it is only an adjective, so cut it. Instead of team player, write what you did in your team. For example, you led a team of four to build a college placement portal. Mistake #2, Words only you understand. Your college project may be called Project Phoenix. Your internal club may be called the CC team. A recruiter has no idea what these mean. Describe the thing in plain words. Not Project Phoenix, but a web app that lets students book lab slots built with Java and MySQL. The third mistake is the one most freshers get wrong, so stay with me. Mistake #3, Numbers with no meaning. Freshers hear that numbers make a resume strong, so they write things like improved performance by 40%. 40% of what compared to what? The recruiter's rule is simple. Your achievement is the number plus what it measures. So write, reduced page load time from five seconds to three seconds in my project, tested on my own laptop. Small and honest is better than big and vague. Mistake #4, A wall of text. Keep it to one page since you are a fresher. Put your skills, projects and education where they are easy to find. Use simple formatting, clear headings and a normal font. Fancy columns, icons and photos can confuse the software that many companies use to read resumes. Save it as a PDF and open it once on your phone because many recruiters will read it there. Every project line should answer three questions. What did you build with which tools and what was the result? Now the mistake that can hurt you after the offer. Lying or stretching? Some freshers add a skill they only watched one video about or round up their marks or change the dates of a gap. Remember that many companies run background verification before you join. They check your education, your dates and sometimes your previous experience. If your resume says one thing and your documents say another, your offer can be put on hold or cancelled. Rules differ from company to company but the risk is real. And even before that, an interviewer will simply ask you about the skill you listed. If you cannot explain it, you lose trust in the whole resume. So the rule is easy. Write only what you can explain for two minutes in an interview and only what your documents can prove. Here is what to do today. Open your resume, cover your name and read it. Delete every adjective that you cannot prove. Rename every insider project name into plain words. Add context to every number. Cut it down to one page. And check every date and mark against your documents. Quick recap. Prove it, explain it in plain words, give numbers a meaning, keep it short and keep it true.
