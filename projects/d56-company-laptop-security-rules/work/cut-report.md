## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your company gives you a laptop on the first day, and three small things you do on it in the first week can get a fresher, a warning, or even a termination without anyone telling you the rule first. If you are waiting to join, or you joined recently, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will explain the three laptop and security rules every company has, why they exist, and what to do instead. Stay till the end, because the third one surprises almost every fresher. First, a simple idea, the laptop you get is not your laptop, it belongs to the company, and so does everything on it, your company laptop usually carries client work, client data, and company mail. Many clients make the company sign a contract that says where their data can go and who can touch it. So, the company watches the laptop closely, this is normal, and it is not about trusting you or not trusting you. Most companies have an information security policy, and you accept it when you join, often by clicking a tick box in the first week. Many freshers click it without reading, but from that moment, any break of that policy is your responsibility, so keep one sentence in mind. The laptop is a work tool, and the rules come from the client, not from your manager. Rule 1. Keep your personal life off the work laptop. That means no personal cloud drives, no personal email, no movies, no games, and no installing software you found online. Many companies block this, and the security team can see when you try. Here is a fresher story, Ravi needed a screen recorder for a class project, so he downloaded a free one on his office laptop. The security tool flagged it the same day, his manager got a mail, and Ravi had to write an explanation in his first month. The fix is easy, use your own phone or personal laptop for personal things. If you need a tool for work, raise a request on the company's software portal, and it is installed for you in a safe way. Asking costs one day, installing it yourself can cost a lot more. Rule 2. Never move work files out of the company systems. It is tempting, you are stuck on a task at night, so you mail the file to your personal email, or you copy code to a pen drive, or you upload it to a personal drive to work from home. It feels harmless because you only want to finish your work. But to the security team, a work file leaving the company is called data leakage, and it is one of the most serious things a person can do. Many companies detect it automatically, and it is a common reason for immediate action. The same goes for screenshots. Do not take a photo of your screen with your phone, and do not share work screenshots on social media or in group chats, even if the screen only looks like a normal page. So what should you do instead? Use the company's approved tools, the company chat, the company drive, and the company remote access. If you cannot access something from home, tell your manager and ask, do not find a shortcut. Rule 3, and this is the one that surprises freshers. The small physical habits count as security rules too. Lock your screen every time you leave your seat, even for 2 minutes. Do not leave the laptop open in the candy. Do not share your password or your badge with a friend who forgot theirs, even if it is your best friend on the team, and never let someone sit and use your logged in laptop. Why is this serious? Because everything done on your account is recorded under your name. If a friend uses your login and does something wrong, the log shows you. Also, be careful with messages that pretend to be from the company. If a mail or a call asks for your password or asks you to click a link to verify your account, stop, the real security team will never ask for your password. Report it to the security mailbox instead. This is the rule that catches people because it feels rude to say no. But saying sorry, company rule is always accepted. So here is what to do now. 3 steps Step 1. In your first week, actually read the information security policy before you tick the box. It is usually only a few pages and it answers most questions. Step 2. If you are not sure whether something is allowed, ask your manager or the security team before you do it. A question is never a mistake. A guess can be. Step 3. Keep work and personal life completely separate on the device in your mail and on your phone. Quick recap. Rule 1. No personal use of the laptop. Rule 2. Never move work files out. Rule 3. Lock your screen and guard your password and badge. Now tell me in the comments. Which of these three did you think was fine before today? Write the number 1, 2 or 3.
