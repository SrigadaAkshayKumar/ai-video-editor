## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your internship can quietly lock you out of every other job, and if the company changes its mind, you may have nothing in writing. This video is for engineering students who have an internship or are about to get one with the promise of a full-time offer. By the end, you will know what a pre-play merchant offer really is, the three things to get clear before you say yes, and how to protect yourself if the offer never comes. First, the basics. PPO means pre-play merchant offer. A company offers you a job based on how you do during your internship, before you even finish your degree. This is becoming more common. A Deloitte India report called Campus Workforce Trends 2026 says that PPO conversion rates have gone up by roughly 6% compared to last year. Experts quoted in The Times of India say companies now use internships as a long interview. A 15- or 30-minute interview is too short to judge you, so they watch you work instead. That is good news. But here is the catch, and it is the thing nobody tells you. Once a student gets a PPO, the college can sign that student out of the campus placement process. The Deloitte partner quoted by The Times of India says it plainly, "After a PPO, you cannot apply to other companies through campus placements. That is called a lock-in." Now think about what that means if the offer is solid, great, but if the company takes it back, you have already missed the campus drives. So number one, before you accept an internship, ask your placement self this exact question. If this company does not give me a PPO, can I sit for other campus drives? Get the answer in an email, not on a phone call. Number two, separate a promise from a document. Here is a reported case. A graduate said on Reddit that they took a six-month internship with the promise of a performance-based offer of 35 lakh rupees a year. The manager reportedly said they would surely get it. Two days before the internship ended, the company allegedly told all 90 interns that nobody would get an offer. The graduate was left with a 6.5 lakh offer from another company. News18 reported this story. This is one person's account, not a confirmed fact about the company. Whether or not every detail is right, the lesson is real. A manager saying you will surely get it is not an offer. So ask the company in an email what is the exact condition for the PPO. Is it a rating? A project? A number of weeks? When will you tell me the decision and will the offer later come before the internship ends? Number three, keep a backup alive and work like it is a real job. Do not stop your own preparation during the internship. Keep practicing coding and aptitude for an hour a day, keep your resume updated, and keep applying to off-campus roles that allow it. If the PPO comes, you lose nothing. If it does not, you are not starting from zero. And inside the internship, make your work easy to see. Write down every task you finish with the result. Ask your mentor for feedback every two weeks, not at the end. At the end of the internship, ask for a short feedback mail and a letter confirming you were there. Those papers help in every interview that follows. One more thing, if you have another offer, do not hide it or lie about it. Read the terms of both and decide with the dates in front of you. So three things. One, ask in writing whether the PPO locks you out of other dives. Two, get the condition and the date for the offer in an email. Three, keep your backup alive and keep proof of your work.
