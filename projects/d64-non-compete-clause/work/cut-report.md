## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Somewhere in your offer letter or joining documents, there may be a clause that says you cannot join a competitor after you leave. Most freshers sign it without reading it, and most freshers do not know whether it can actually stop them. If you are about to join your first job, or you are one year in and thinking of switching, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will explain the two clauses people mix up what an Indian court recently said about one of them and three things to check before you sign. Stay till the end, because the third check is the one that protects your next job. First, the two clauses people mix up. The first is a minimum service clause. It says you must stay for a fixed period, and if you leave early, you pay an amount. This one works during your job. The second is a non-compete or non-solicit clause. It says that after you leave, you cannot join a competitor, or you cannot take clients or colleagues with you for a few years. This one works after your job. Same contract, two very different clauses, and the law treats them very differently. Now, what does the law say? Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act says that any agreement that stops a person from practising a lawful profession, trade, or business is to that extent void. Courts have read this in a simple way. Rules that apply while you are still employed are usually allowed. Rules that apply after your job has ended are usually void. In May 2025, the Supreme Court upheld a clause in a bank's recruitment rules. Staff had to serve for a minimum of three years or pay two lakh rupees. The court said it was allowed because it applied during employment and did not stop the person from working elsewhere later. But the court also said its ruling should not be treated as the final word for every case. Now look at the other side. In a recent case, a software engineer signed an agreement with a clause that blocked him from working with business partners of his employer for three years after leaving. He resigned, served a three-month notice period, and joined one of those partners. His old company went to court to stop him. The Delhi High Court said the clause was void under Section 27 because it tried to control his work after the job ended. And the company had not shown a real secret or proprietary interest to protect. The court said that if the company suffered any loss, that could be dealt with through damages, but he could keep his new job. So here is the catch. Even when the clause is not enforceable, you can still be dragged into a dispute. And a court can still stop you from sharing confidential information. Here are the three checks. One. Find the clause and read the words. Look for the terms non-compete, non-solicit, restrictive, covenant, or exclusivity. Note how long it lasts and what it covers. Two. Separate during-job rules from after-job rules. A rule that you must serve a minimum period is a during-job rule. A rule that you cannot join a competitor for years after leaving is an after-job rule. If you are not sure which one it is, ask HR in writing, politely. Three. The one that protects your next job. Never take client lists, source code, documents, or customer data with you. Even if a non-compete is void, taking confidential material is a separate problem and it is the one companies actually win cases on. What should you do this week? If you have already signed, open your offer letter or employment agreement and search for those four words. Keep a copy of the signed document in your own email so you can read it anytime. If you have not signed yet, you can ask HR what a clause means. That is a normal question and it will not cancel your offer. And remember, this video is general information, not legal advice. Of course, look at the facts of each case. So, if a clause worries you, speak to a lawyer before you act. So, to sum up. A minimum service clause works during your job. A non-compete after your job is ordinarily void under Section 27, but your confidential data is still protected. Read the clause, separate the two and take nothing that is not yours.
