## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

The interview is going well, then the interviewer smiles and says, "Do you have any questions for us?" and you say, "No sir, everything is clear." If you are a fresher giving technical or HR interviews in the next 5 minutes, I will explain why that one answer quietly hurts you. The three questions that make you look serious and the two questions you must never ask. Stay till the end because the third question is the one most freshers get wrong. First, why do interviewers ask this at the end? It is not a formality, it is the last test. They are checking three things, did you listen during the interview, are you curious about the work and do you actually want this job or any job? Think about it from their side. If a candidate has no question at all, it can look like they did not think about the role. It does not mean you will be rejected for it. But when two candidates are close, the one who asked a good question is the one people remember. The good news is, you do not need clever questions. You need three simple ones prepared before the day. Question number one. What would a good first three months look like for someone in this role? This shows you are already thinking about doing the job well. Not only about getting it. And the answer is useful for you. It tells you what the team really expects from a fresher. Whether that is learning the tools, finishing small tasks, or supporting the senior engineers. If it is a campus drive and the interviewer is an HR person, you can ask it a little differently. What does the training and onboarding look like after joining? Listen to the answer and if you can connect it to something you already know. For example, you can say that sounds like what I did in my final year project where I had to learn a new tool quickly. Question number two. What kind of work does the team do day to day and what technologies do you use? This is the question that shows genuine interest. Freshers often walk into an interview knowing only the company name. When you ask about the real work, you stand out. But here is the catch. Do your homework first. If the answer is already on the careers page and you ask it, it looks like you did not prepare. So read the job description before you go and ask something that goes one step beyond it. For example, instead of asking, do you use cloud? You can ask, how does the team decide which task a new joiner picks up first? Question number three. The one most freshers get wrong. What are the next steps in the process and when can I expect to hear back? Freshers often avoid this because they are afraid it sounds pushy. It does not. It is polite and it is practical. Without it, you leave the room, not knowing if there is another round, a document stage or a result in two days or two weeks. Say it calmly. I understand you will decide after all interviews. When should I expect an update and who should I contact if I do not hear back? Write the answer down. If the date passes, you now have a reason to send a polite follow up message instead of waiting in silence. Now, two questions you must never ask in this interview. First, never ask about salary, leave or work from home in a first round unless the interview brings it up. You will have time for those after you get the offer. Asking too early can make it look like these are the only things you care about. Second, never ask something that sounds like doubt about the interviewer. For example, will I even get selected or did I answer badly? That puts pressure on them and it makes you look unsure. If you feel nervous and your mind goes blank, you can keep one safe line ready. Thank you for your time. I would love to know what you look for in someone who does well on this team. So, to sum up, when they ask, do you have any questions? Never say no. Ask what a good first three months looks like. Ask about the day-to-day work and ask about the next steps. Skip salary and doubts. Now, I want to hear from you guys. Comment the interview question that confused you the most and I will help you with an answer. If this helped, like the video and subscribe so you don't miss the next update.
