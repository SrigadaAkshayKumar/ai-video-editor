## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

A real offer letter never asks you for money, not one rupee. Yet, every year, students and freshers pay likes of rupees for offers that do not exist. If you are looking for your first job in software or waiting for a joining letter, this video is for you. By the end, you will know five red flags that expose a fake offer, the one check that takes two minutes and what to do if you have already paid. Stay for the third red flag because it fools even smart engineers. First, a real case. So, you know, this is not just a warning. In January, this year, the Cyberabad police arrested five people for a job fraud racket. A job seeker in Hyderabad looking for a software job got a message on WhatsApp from a woman who promised a place in a repeated multinational company. He paid 3,20,000 rupees by UPI. Then, he got an offer letter in the name of a real company and even an office address. He joined, but no salary came. After some weeks, he received a termination letter and his access was shut. Nobody would pay him or refund him. Police said the gang had made fake email addresses that looked almost like the real companies, held sham interviews and fake training sessions, and even set up fake offices. They asked job seekers for 2,000,000 rupees for what they called backdoor placements. Remember this phrase, "backdoor placement, if anyone says it to you, the conversation is already over." Now, the five red flags. Red flag 1. They ask for money, registration fee, training fee, laptop deposit, security deposit, document verification charge. The name changes. The trap is the same. Genuine companies pay you. You do not pay them. Red flag 2. The email address looks right, but it is not. Look closely at the part after the ad sign. A real company writes from its own domain. A fake one uses a free mail address or a name with one extra letter or a dash. Always read it slowly, letter by letter. Red flag 3. The one that fooled smart people, you really did attend an interview and it felt real. There was a video call, questions, even a technical round. Fake recruiters copy this because once you have invested your time, you trust them. A real-looking interview proves nothing. Only the official source proves something. Red flag 4. Pressure. Pay today or the seat goes to someone else. Real hiring has deadlines, but never a deadline attached to a payment. Red flag 5. They only talk on WhatsApp or Telegram from a personal number and avoid a proper company call or email thread. Real recruiters use official channels and they are happy for you to verify them. So, how do you check in two minutes? Step 1. Open the company's official website yourself. Do not click the link in the message. Go to the careers page and look for a notice about fake offers. Most big companies publish one and many say clearly that they never charge for jobs. Step 2. Find the company's official hiring email or helpline from that website and write to them with the name, the role and the offer reference number. Do not use the contact details in the letter itself. Step 3. If you are a student, ask your placement cell. If the offer came from a campus drive, they will know. If the offer did not come through the campus, ask why. Step 4. Check the offer itself. Does the letter name, a joining date, a location and a reporting person is the salary structure written out. Fake letters are often vague or full of spelling mistakes or the company name is slightly off. If any one of these steps fails, stop. Do not pay, do not share documents and do not argue with them. What if you have already paid or shared your documents? Do not feel ashamed. These gangs fool engineers, teachers and parents. What matters is speed. Call 1930 right away. That is the national cybercrime helpline and the police in the Hyderabad case also ask people to use it along with the national cybercrime reporting portal. The earlier you report, the better the chance that the bank can hold the money. Keep everything. Screenshots of chats, the offer letter, payment receipts, the phone numbers and the email addresses. Do not delete anything and do not block them before you take screenshots and tell your bank. Ask them to flag the transaction. Then change the passwords of any account where you share details. To sum up, a real job never costs you money. Check the email address, ignore the pressure, do not trust an interview alone and verify only from the company's own website.
