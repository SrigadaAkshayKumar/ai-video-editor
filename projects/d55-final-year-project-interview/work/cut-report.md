## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your interviewer says, "Tell me about your final year project," and 90% of freshers make the same mistake in the next ten seconds. They start reading out the title. If you are a final year student or a fresher with interviews coming, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will give you a simple four-part answer you can use for any project. The questions interviewers ask next and the three mistakes that make a good project sound like a copied one. Stay till the end because the third mistake can end your interview even when your project is real. First, why do interviewers ask about your project at all? It is not to hear the title. For a fresher with no work experience, the project is the only real work the interviewer can discuss. In two minutes, they check three things. Did you actually build it? Do you understand what you used? And can you explain a technical thing in simple words? Because that is what you will do with your team every day. So, the interviewer is not judging how big your project is. A simple project that you explain clearly beats a big project that you cannot explain. Here is the answer structure. Four parts, about sixty seconds in total. Part one, the problem. One sentence on what problem it solves and for whom? For example, students in our college waste time finding free classrooms. So, we built a small web app that shows which rooms are empty. Part two, what you used. Name the main tools in one sentence. The language, the framework, the database. Do not list everything. Part three, your role. This is the most important part. Say clearly what you built yourself. For example, I built the login and the room search and my teammate built the admin page. Part four, the result or what you learned. One real outcome, it worked for 200 students or it was hard to make it fast. So, I added a cache then stop. Let the interviewer pick the next question. Now, the questions that come next because they will come. Expect these, why did you choose this language or database? What was the hardest bug? What would you do differently? What happens if 10,000 users use it at once? You do not need a perfect answer. You need an honest one. I chose Python because I already knew it. But for more users, I would look at a faster option is a good answer. It shows you understand the limits. And prepare one story before the interview. A real bug, what you tried and how you fixed it. The hardest bug story is the most asked and the most remembered. Now, the three mistakes. Mistake number one, reading the abstract. If your answer sounds like the report you submitted to college, the interviewer will notice. Speak the way you would explain it to a friend from another branch. Mistake number two, saying, we for everything, we built it, we tested it, we deployed it, the interviewer thinks. So, what did you do? Use we for the team and I for your own part. It is not bragging. It is the only way they can judge you. And here is mistake number three, the one that ends interviews, claiming work that is not yours. Maybe you bought the project from a website or copied it from a senior or you only ran code a teammate wrote. Interviewers have seen it many times. They ask one small question like, show me how this function works or why did you write it this way? And the answer falls apart. If part of the project was copied or taken from a tutorial, say it. I followed a tutorial for the first version. Then I added the search and the filters myself. That sounds honest and strong. Getting caught sounds like lying and a company that finds out later during background verification or after joining can take it much more seriously. So, the rule is simple. Only talk deeply about what you can open and explain today. So, here is what to do this week. Pick your best two projects, write the four part answer for each, then say it out loud and time it. Because writing and speaking are different skills. Then open the code and be ready to explain every file you list on your resume. To sum up, problem, tools, your role, result, use "I" for your part.
