## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your first appraisal can be decided months before anyone tells you about it, and most freshers find that out when it is already too late. If you are about to join your first job, or you are in your first year, stay with me. By the end of this video, you will know the three things that decide your rating and the one mistake that quietly cause freshers a good hike. First, how an appraisal works in most software companies. Details differ from company to company, so, always check your own policy. But the pattern is very similar. Your manager writes goals for you usually at the start of the year, or soon after your project allocation. During the year, you do the work. Near the end of the cycle, you write a self-review. Your manager gives a rating, and then a larger group compares ratings across the team. That last step is the one freshers never see. The final rating then feeds into your hike and your bonus if your company pays one. Many companies also treat a new joiner's first cycle differently. For example, by counting only the months you have worked. So, ask your HR how the first cycle works for you. Reason #1 Your Goals Many freshers never read them. Your goals are the exam paper. If nobody has written them down, you are being judged on a paper you have never seen. So, in your first month, ask your manager one simple question. What does a good year look like for me? Then write the answer in an email and send it back. So, it is on record. Ravi joined worked hard on whatever was asked and learnt in December that his goals were about testing. While he had spent months on development, his effort was real, but it did not match the paper. So, if your goals look unclear or too big, say so early and politely. A goal that is fixed in March is easy. A goal that is questioned in December sounds like an excuse. Thing #2 Proof Your manager remembers the last few weeks, not the whole year. So, keep a simple work diary. Every Friday, write three lines, what you finished, what you learned and who you helped. Keep it in a personal note, not on company property and never copy confidential material. When the self-preview opens, you will not be staring at a blank box. You will have 50 small facts instead of one vague sentence like "I worked hard", there is a bonus. This diary also helps in your next interview because you can say exactly what you built and what you fixed. Freshers who can name real tasks sound far more confident than freshers who say "I did some work on a project". Thing #3 Is Visibility And this is where the costly mistake hides. The mistake is staying silent. Managers think good work will speak for itself. But the people who decide your rating in the team discussion may never have seen your work. Only your manager speaks for you in that room, so give your manager the material. Send a short update mail every two weeks, two or three lines. What is done? What is blocked? What is next? It also helps with the timesheet habit we covered in an earlier video. And one more trap. Do not walk into the review with only a complaint about your hike, walk in with your goals, your proof and your next year plan. That conversation goes very differently. So to sum up, know your goals, keep your proof and make your work visible. These three habits cost you 10 minutes a week and they can change your rating.
