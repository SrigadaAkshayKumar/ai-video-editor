## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your interviewer asks a technical question, and your mind goes completely blank. What you do in the next ten seconds can decide the whole interview. If you are a fresher preparing for your first job, stay with me. By the end of this video, you'll know exactly what to say when you do not know the answer, and the one reply that almost always ends the round. First, understand what the interviewer is really checking. For a fresher, they do not expect you to know everything. They are checking how you think when you are stuck, because in the job, you'll be stuck every single day. Most freshers do one of two things, they go silent for a full minute, or they guess loudly and confidently with a wrong answer. Both hurt you. Silence looks like panic. A confident wrong answer looks worse, because the interviewer cannot trust anything else you say. Here is a small story. Two freshers were asked the same question about how a web page talks to a server. Ravi stayed silent, and then guessed the wrong answer with full confidence. Meena said, "I have not built this myself, but I think the browser sends a request, and the server sends back a reply. Let me think through the steps. She was only half right, but the interviewer helped her finish the answer, and she got the offer. Same question, very different result." So, there is a third way, and it has three steps. Now, the reply I promised, it is "I don't know," and then nothing else. Just a flat "no," and you stop talking, think about it. That sentence gives the interviewer nothing to work with. So, they move to the next question, or they end the topic, saying "I don't know" is fine. Stopping there is the mistake. Let me show you what to say instead. Step one. Say honestly. What you do know that is close. For example, I have not used that exact feature, but I know the basic idea behind it. Step two. Think aloud. Say, "Let me try to work it out." Then walk through it slowly. For example, if they ask about a database index you have not studied, you could say, "I think it is like the index at the back of a book." So, the database can find a row without reading every row. Even a partly right idea shows your mind is working. Step three. Ask for a hint, politely. Say, "Am I thinking in the right direction?" Interviewers often give a small clue, and what you do with that clue is what they notice. Here is the trap. Never make up facts. If you are not sure, say "I'm not sure," but I think that one phrase keeps you honest and safe. Blanks will still happen, so prepare for them. Pick the subjects you wrote on your resume, and for each one, prepare to explain three things in simple words. What is it? Why is it used? And where did you use it? Then practise answering out loud, not in your head, out loud to a friend or to your phone camera. Freshers who only read answers silently freeze, because speaking is a different skill. And remember, a question you could not answer is useful. Right after the interview, write it down, look it up, and next time you will own it. Here is what to do today. Choose three topics from your resume. For each one, explain it out loud in 30 seconds. Then, practice the three steps once, say what you know, think aloud, and ask for a hint. Quick recap, never go silent, never guess loudly, and never stop at "I don't know." Now, tell me in the comments, guys.
