## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

300 people lost their jobs at one big software company because of a side income, not for stealing, not for bad work. For a second job, nobody told the company about. This video is for every fresher who is about to join a software company or has just joined. By the end, you will know what a second job is called, which side work is usually safe, the one line in your contract to check first, and the three steps to follow before you earn a single rupee on the side. First, the word moonlighting means doing another job or paid project on the side, usually without your main employer knowing. During the work-from-home years, many people started taking side projects after office hours. In September 2022, Wipro terminated about 300 employees and its chairman called it cheating. Other big companies, including TCS and Infosys, publicly said they were against hiding a second job. Now, here is the catch. The problem was never the extra money. The problem was the word hidden. Keep that word in mind because the whole video depends on it. Step one. Open your offer letter and your employment agreement and look for three words. Exclusive conflict of interest and non-compete. Most traditional software contracts say you must work only for that company. If yours says so, a second job can be treated as a breach of that contract and courts have allowed companies to terminate people found doing it. You will also hear that a law called the Factories Act bans double employment. But it is not a safe shield for you because some states exempt software companies from it. So, do not build your plan on what you heard in a group chat. Build it on your own contract. Step two. Know which kind of side work you are talking about because they are not the same. The first kind is a competing job. Working for a rival company or for your own company's client? This is the one that gets people terminated. Never do it. The second kind is a paid side project in your own time, such as freelance design, tutoring, or building an app. This is a grey area. Some companies allow it, but only with written permission. The third kind is something with no employer at all, like learning open source for practice or a college project. This is almost always safe, as long as you use no company laptop, no company code, and no company time. Here is where freshers go wrong. They think working after office hours makes it automatically fine. It does not. Time is not the rule. Conflict and permission are the rule. Step three. The three steps before you earn anything. One. Read the policy. Ask HR or look on the company portal for a policy on outside work or gig work. After the controversy, Infosys said employees may take up gig work but only with the manager's prior consent and only if it does not compete with the company or its clients. Tech Mahindra spoke of a similar written approval process. Policies differ, so check yours. Two. Ask in writing. Send a short mail to your manager and HR describing the work, the hours, and that it has no link to your clients. A verbal yes does not protect you. A mail does Three. Keep it clean. No company laptop, no company data, no work during office hours, and stop immediately if the company says no. Remember, the third mistake is not the one most people expect. It is the quiet one. Using your office laptop just once for a side project is enough to turn a permitted gig into a policy violation. Let us recap. Moonlighting is a hidden second job and hidden is what gets people terminated. Check your contract, know which kind of side work it is, and ask in writing before you earn a single rupee.
