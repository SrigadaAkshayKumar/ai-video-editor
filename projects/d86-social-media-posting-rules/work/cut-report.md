## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

You got the offer letter, and your first thought was to post it. Stop. One photo of that letter can leak your salary, your home address, and even get your offer question. This video is for freshers who are about to join a tech company or who have just joined. In the next five minutes, you will learn the three posting mistakes that can hurt you, what is safe to share, and a ten-second check to do before every post. Stay till the end, because the third mistake is the one most freshers never notice. First, why this matters? Your offer letter is not just a happy moment. It is a private document. It usually carries your full name, your address, your date of birth, your pay structure, and often a candidate or reference number. Anyone can screenshot a public post. Scammers collect such details to make fake calls that sound real. And the company can see your post too. The recruiters, the managers, and the colleagues you will work with, they all use the same platforms. So, the question is not whether you can celebrate, you can. The question is how to celebrate without handing over your details. Mistake number one, posting a photo of the offer letter, the email, or the joining kit. Many offer letters say the terms are confidential. Even when yours does not say it, the pay and the reference number are your private details. A blurred photo is not enough because blur can often be undone and the top and bottom of the page still show the layout. Here is a safer way, write the post in your own words, say which company, which role, and that you are excited. No photo of the letter. If you want a picture, use a plain photo of yourself or a graphic you made and never share your offer letter in a group chat for likes. Groups get forwarded. Mistake number two, sharing work details once you join. It starts small, a photo of your desk with a screen visible, a selfie in the office with a whiteboard behind you, a story showing your project name, your client's name, or a code editor. To you, it is just content. To your company, it can be client information and client information is the most protected thing in a tech company. Many companies have a social media policy in the employee handbook and you usually accept it in your first week, read it. The safe rule is simple, show yourself, not the work, take the photo where no screen, no whiteboard, no paper, and no badge number is visible. Never name a client or a project unless the company has already announced it publicly. If you are unsure, ask your manager one line, "Is it fine if I post a photo from the office?" That one question makes you look careful, not scared. Mistake number three, and this is the one I promised. It is what you say, not what you show. Complaining about your manager, your team, the training, or the salary, even on a private looking account, freshers think a close friend's list or a fake name makes it safe. It does not screenshots travel, and during background verification and later reference checks, your public profile can be looked at. A rant written in frustration can't sit there for years. There is a second side to it. Do not argue in comments about your company, even to defend it. You are not the official voice of the company, and a wrong statement can create trouble for you. If something is really wrong at work, use the proper door. Your manager, your HR contact, or the company's grievance channel. A post is never the first step. So, what is safe to post? Safe that you got placed in your own words. A thank you to teachers and friends. What you learned in training, in general terms. Your own certificates and your own projects which belong to you, not safe. The document itself, your pay, client or project names, internal tools, internal emails, and anything your handbook says is confidential. Before every post, do the 10 second check. 1. Does it show any document or number that is mine alone? 2. Does it show anything that belongs to the company or a client? 3. Would I be comfortable if my manager and an HR person read it tomorrow? If any answer is no, change the post or do not post it. So, to sum up, celebrate in your own words, never post a letter, never show client work, and never rant online.
