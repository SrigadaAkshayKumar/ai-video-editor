## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your joining date is fixed, your project is not, and in some companies, a fresher can sit without a project for months. If you are about to join your first job in software, this video is for you. By the end, you will know what the bench really is, what one big company's new rule tells you, and the four things to do in your first weeks, so you get a project faster. Stay for the third one, because it is the one almost nobody does. First, what is the bench? In a services company, you are usually paid by the company, but the client pays the company only when you work on a client project. When you are between projects, you are on the bench. For a fresher, it often happens right after training. You have finished learning the joining formalities are done, and now you wait for a project to be allotted the way it can be days in some cases and much longer in others. It depends on your skills, on demand, and on your company. Being on the bench is not a punishment, but it is not a holiday either, and companies watch it closely. Now, the news that shows why this matters. Local has announced a new bench policy, effective from the 1st of October. Employees in band 2 and below can stay on the bench for up to 180 calendar days, that is about 6 months. The clock starts when you are released from a billable project or returned from onsite without a new billable role. The catch is in the same report. Employees who do not get an allocation within that time can be separated under the terms of their employment contract. Read that again, even the generous version of the rule has an end date, and reports say the same article that praised Vipro also said other big companies keep the bench much tighter. So whichever company you join, do not treat the bench as free time. I am telling you this has a reported policy of one company, yours may be different. So ask, here are the 4 things to do. 1. In your first week, ask your trainer or manager how project allocation works. Who decides what does the company look at, which skills are in demand right now. A short polite question puts you on their radar. 2. Use the bench time like a job, finish every training module and every assessment on time. Many companies look at training scores and completion when they choose people for projects. 3. This is the one nobody does. Pick the skill that your likely project needs and build one small thing with it. A small working project, a certificate that is actually relevant, anything you can show. When someone says we need a person who knows this, you want to be the person who already tried it. 4. Keep a short weekly update and send it to your manager or resource coordinator. 3 lines. What I learned, what I built, that I am available for allocation. Be useful, not noisy. Now the trap. Do not sit and wait for the mail that says you are allocated. Do not spend the whole bench period on reels. And do not panic and take a random project in a skill you do not want without asking what you will learn there. Ask first, then decide. Also read your offer letter and your company's policy for the words bench, deployment and separation. So you know what your company really allows to sum up. The bench is the gap between training and a client project. Wipro's new policy gives a long window to junior staff but even that window ends. Ask how allocation works, finish training strongly, build one relevant skill and send a short weekly update.
