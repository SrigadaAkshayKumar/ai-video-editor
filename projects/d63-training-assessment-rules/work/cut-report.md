## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your joining date is not the finish line. In many companies, there is an exam waiting for you in the first training weeks and failing, it can end your job. If you are a fresher waiting to join, this video is for you. In the next 5 minutes, I will explain what the training assessment is, the 3 things that decide whether you clear it and what to do before day 1. Stay till the end, because the third thing is the one most freshers realize too late. First, let us look at a real case. In February 2025, news outlets reported that Infosys had let go of hundreds of trainees at its Mysuru campus, because they had not cleared internal assessments. The outlets did not agree on the number. Some said over 300. A trade union said much more. So, I will not quote one. But the company's own statement was clear. Infosys said that all freshers, after foundational training, are expected to clear internal assessments. They get 3 attempts. If they fail, they cannot continue with the organization and the company said this is also mentioned in their contract. Now, this is one company and rules differ everywhere. But the lesson is for all of us. The training period is not a holiday, it is part of the selection and the rule is often written in the very papers you sign Thing No. 1. Read the clause before you need it. Open your offer letter and any joining agreement and look for words like training, assessment, confirmation or termination during training. Find 3 answers. How many attempts do you get? What is the passing mark? And what happens if you do not clear it? Many freshers never read this part, because on the day of joining, there are 20 papers to sign. Do it now while you have time and keep a photo of the pages. If anything is unclear, ask the recruiter by email and keep the reply. Do not trust a senior story about how easy it was. Rules change by company, by year and by role. Thing No. 2. Prepare the basics before day 1. Training assessments usually test fundamentals. Think programming basics, databases, a little web and logic. They test what you were taught in college but under time pressure and often in an online format. Here is a simple plan. If you have 6 weeks, spend the first 2 on one programming language, the next 2 on databases and simple queries and the last 2 on time practice tests. 30-45 minutes a day is enough if you do it every day. But here is the catch. Do not collect 10 courses. Pick one language, one database and finish them. Depth beats a long list. Thing No. 3. The one most freshers realize too late. What do you do after a bad attempt? Most people go silent, they feel ashamed and they study the same way again. That is the mistake. After a bad attempt, do 3 things in the same week. First, ask your trainer or mentor which topics cost you marks. Second, rebuild your plan around only those topics, not everything. Third, practice in the same format as the test with a timer. Do not hide a failed attempt from yourself and never copy answers or use shortcuts in the assessment. Cheating can end a career faster than a low score. A second attempt is a chance so treat it like the first day of an exam, not the last. So here is your plan for this week. Today, read your offer letter and joining papers for the training and assessment clause. Tomorrow, pick one language and one database and fix a daily time. By the weekend, take one time test and write down your week topics. And if you are already in training, do not wait for the result. Ask early, practice daily and treat every weekly test like the real one. To sum up, training is part of the job so read the clause, prepare the basics and fix a bad attempt in the same week.
