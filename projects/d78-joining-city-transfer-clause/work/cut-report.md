## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

You got the offer, you are happy, and then the joining email arrives, and the city on it is not the city you wanted. If you are a fresher waiting to join an information technology company, this video is for you. By the end, you will know where your work location is really decided, what the mobility line in your offer letter allows, and the one mistake that makes a transfer request fail. The third point is the one most freshers learn too late. First, who actually decides where you work. At most large companies, your location is decided by the business and not by the recruiter. The company looks at where the training batch is running, which project needs people, and where they are seats and equipment. Your preference matters, but it is only one input. Many offer letters carry a line that says the company can post you to any of its offices or client sites in India or abroad, as business needs require. Some call it a mobility clause, some call it a transfer clause. So, when HR says, we will try to give you your preferred city, that is a polite intention. It is not a promise. The only thing that is a promise is what is written in the offer letter. Now, the open loop from the start. Pull out your offer letter and look for three lines. Line 1, the work location, some letters name, a city for training, and a different city for the first posting. Read both line 2, the transfer or mobility clause. If it says any location, then a posting to another city is allowed by your own signature. Line 3, anything about relocation support. Some companies give temporary accommodation or a relocation allowance for the first weeks and some give nothing. If it is not written, do not assume it. If a line is unclear, write a short polite mail to HR and ask. Do it before the joining date, not after you have already paid a deposit for a flat. Let me tell you about a fresher I will call Ravi. He was posted to a city far from home and in his second week sent a long message to his manager saying he could not live there and wanted a transfer immediately. It failed. Not because transfers are impossible, but because of how he asked. Here is the mistake. He asked before he had done anything for the project. A manager has no reason to move someone who has not yet delivered a single task and a new joiner who asks to live on week 2 looks like a risk. Another fresher in the same batch waited, finished her training assessment to count her first tasks and only then asked politely with a reason and a date. She got a hearing. So how should you ask if you genuinely need a different city? Step 1, finish your training and your first assessments properly. Your record is your bargaining power. Step 2, have a real reason such as a family medical situation and keep a document ready if it is true. Never invent a reason. Background checks and manager conversations can expose it and that can hurt you far more than a posting you do not like. Step 3, ask your manager first in person or on a call and then confirm by a short mail. Say what you need, why and that you will keep delivering until a decision is made. Step 4, ask whether there is an open seat in that city. A transfer needs a place to land and a request that names a real team or office is much easier to approve. And remember, you can always ask but the company can say no. Plan for that answer too. Here is what to do this week. Open your offer letter and find the three lines. Do not sign a rent agreement before your location is final. Keep your documents ready in case you need to move on short notice and if the city is not your first choice, give it three months of honest work before you decide anything. Quick recap location is decided by business need. The mobility line in your offer letter allows a posting anywhere and a transfer request works only after you have shown your work.
