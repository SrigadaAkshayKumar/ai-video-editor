## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

The interview is going well then the interviewer smiles and says, "Do you have any questions for us?" and you say, "No sir, everything is clear." If you are a fresher giving interviews, in the next 5 minutes I will show you why that one answer quietly costs people offers. The 3 questions that make you look serious and the 2 questions you must never ask. Stay till the end because the third question works even in an HR round. First why do they ask this at all? It is not a formality, the interviewer is not looking for information. They are looking at you. Do you care about the work? Did you listen during the interview? Can you think on the spot? Think about it from their side. Two freshers have similar marks and similar answers. One says, "No questions." The other asks a sharp question about the team. Who sounds more interested? The second one, "Every time." And here is the catch, you do not need a clever question. You need a genuine one, asked in a calm voice. Prepare it before the interview, not during question number 1. What would a fresher in this role work on in the first 3 months? This shows that you are already thinking about doing the job, not just getting the job. The answer also helps you. You learn if it is development, testing, support or training first. You can say it like this, "Sir, if I join this team what would my first 3 months look like? What should I be ready to learn?" Listen to the answer and nod. If they mention a tool or a language, remember it, you can use it in your next sentence which is a strong move. Question number 2. What does a good fresher do well in your team? This is the same idea as asking what success looks like. It tells the interviewer that you want to meet their standard and it gives you a free preview of what they value. Maybe it is learning fast, maybe it is asking questions early, maybe it is reliability. You can also ask, "How big is the team?" and "Who would I learn from?" A real person, a buddy or a mentor makes the first months much easier. Keep it short. One question, one follow up at most. This is not a second interview where you grill them. Question number 3. The one that works in every round. What are the next steps and when can I expect to hear from you? It sounds simple but it does 3 things. It shows you are organized, it ends the interview on a polite confident note and it saves you from waiting in the dark for weeks wondering what is happening. If they give a time, write it down. If nobody replies by then, you now have a fair reason to send a short, polite follow up mail in an HR round. This is the best question. The HR person can actually answer it unlike questions about technical work. Now the 2 questions you must never ask in a first interview. 1. How much salary will I get and how many leaves? Not in the technical round, money and leave can be discussed when the company sends an offer or if HR brings it up. Asking too early makes it sound like that is all you care about. 2. A question you could have answered with one search. Like what does your company do? That tells the interviewer you did not prepare and it can undo a good interview. So the rule is simple. Ask about the work, the team and the next step. Leave the money and the googling for later. So to sum up, when they ask if you have questions never say none. Ask what the first 3 months look like. Ask what a good fresher does well and ask about the next steps. Never ask about salary too early and never ask what a search could answer. Now I want to hear from you guys.
