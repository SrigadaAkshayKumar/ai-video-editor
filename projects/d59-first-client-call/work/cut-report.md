## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your first client call can go wrong in the first ten seconds, and it usually has nothing to do with your technical skills. If you are a fresher about to join a tech company or already in your first few months, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will give you the three rules for sitting in a client call and one sentence that saves you when you do not know the answer. Stay till the end, because the third rule is the one most freshers never hear. First, Why These Calls Feel So Scary. In a service company, the client is the one who pays for the project. Your manager answers to them, and your team is judged by how the client feels. Most freshers are not expected to speak much in the first few calls. Usually, you are added to listen, to learn, and to take notes. But people notice how you behave, even when you say nothing. So, the goal of your first call is simple. Look prepared, stay calm, and do not make a promise you cannot keep. Rule number one, prepare for ten minutes before the call. Open the meeting invite and read the agenda. If there is no agenda, ask your manager one line, "What is this call about?" Then, open the ticket or document that will be discussed and read it once. Next, check the boring things. Is your microphone working? Is your internet stable? Is your background clean? Are your chat notifications turned off so that no private message pops up on a shared screen and write down the names of the people on the call with their roles. When you can say "Thanks Priya" for the clarification, you sound like part of the team. Rule number two, say less and write more during the call. Keep your microphone on mute unless you are speaking. Keep your camera on if the team does. Do not interrupt when someone finishes. Wait one second and then speak. Keep a notes file open. Write down every decision, every date, and every task with a name next to it. If a task has your name, write it exactly as the client said it. After the call, send a short summary mail or chat to your manager within the hour. For example, "Hi, here are my notes from the call. One, the client wants the report fixed by Friday. Two, I will check the error logs today. Please correct me if I missed anything." This one habit makes freshers stand out. Your manager sees that you listened and that you close the loop. Now rule number three, the one I promised, never promise a date, a feature, or a fix on the call unless your manager has already agreed to it. It feels natural to say, "Yes sir, I will finish it by tomorrow." You want to look confident, but if you are wrong, the client remembers that promise and it becomes your problem and your manager's problem. Here is the sentence that saves you. Thanks for the question. Let me check this with the team and I will get back to you by end of day today. Say it calmly and then do it. It sounds so simple, but it tells the client two things. You are careful and you keep your word. That is exactly what a client wants from a team member. So here is what to do from today. Before the call, ten minutes of preparation, during the call, mute, listen and take notes. After the call, send a summary within the hour and whenever you are asked something you are not sure about, check first and promise later. Quick recap.
