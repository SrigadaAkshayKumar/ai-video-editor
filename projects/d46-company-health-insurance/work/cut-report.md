## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your company health insurance can disappear on your last working day, and most freshers find that out in a hospital queue. If you are about to join, or just joined, your first tech job, this video is for you. By the end, you will know what group health insurance really covers, three mistakes that cost freshers their claims, and the one thing to do before you ever resign. Stay till the end because the third mistake is the one that quietly cancels your protection. First, the basics in plain words. Group health insurance is one policy that your company buys from an insurance company for all its employees. Usually, the company pays the premium, you do not buy it, and you do not get a separate policy in your own name. What exactly is covered depends on your company's plan, the hospital network, the cover amount, and whether your parents or spouse are included are all decided by the company. So, never copy what your friend in another company told you, ask your own HR for the policy document, or open the benefits page in your employee portal. And here is the catch. This cover generally stays active only while you work for that company. Mistake number one. Assuming that every bill will be paid. Many policies have limits, there can be a cap on room charges, a limit on certain treatments, and a list of hospitals where treatment is cashless. If you go to a hospital outside the network, you may have to pay first and claim the money later. So, do this in your first week, find your cover amount, check whether your parents are included, and save the list of network hospitals near your home on your phone. Also, save the insurance helpline number. In an emergency, nobody has time to search for it. Mistake number two. Hiding or skipping medical details. Some forms ask about existing health conditions. Maybe you have asthma, thyroid, or high blood pressure. It feels easier to leave it blank. Do not insurance works on honest disclosure. Under the rules set by the insurance regulator, after five continuous years of a policy, the insurer generally cannot reject a claim for a genuine unintentional miss. That period was reduced from eight years to five in 2024. But the rule does not protect you if you hide a serious illness on purpose. In that case, claims can still be rejected even after five years. And notice the word continuous. The five years only count if your cover has no break. Remember that because it matters in the next mistake. Now, mistake number three. The quiet one, you resign, serve your notice, and on your last day the group cover stops. If you join a new company next month, there may be a gap. And if you take a break, you are completely uninsured exactly when a medical bill would hurt the most. Here is what you can do. First, before you resign, ask your insurer or HR whether you can move from the group policy to an individual or family policy. Reports say this is generally possible only with the same insurer, only if there is no gap in cover. And the insurer decides the terms and the premium. Waiting periods you have already completed can carry over but only when the cover is continuous. Second and better, buy a small personal health policy early while you are young and healthy. The premium is usually low at your age. And that policy keeps running whichever company you work for. Premiums you pay for your own policy can also give a tax benefit under Section 80D. But check with a tax professional whether your chosen tax regime allows it. So here is your plan for this week. One, download your insurance policy document and note the cover amount and who is included. Two, save the network hospitals near your home and the helpline number on your phone. Three, answer every health question honestly and keep a copy of what you submitted. Four, before you ever resign, talk to your insurer about what happens next. Do not wait for the last day. Let us recap. Company health insurance is a great benefit but it is not forever and it is not unlimited. Know what is covered, be honest in the form and never let your cover break.
