## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your offer letter is not a job, not yet, and thousands of freshers learn that the hard way, after they have already told everyone and stopped looking for other options. This video is for every student or fresher holding a software offer or waiting for one. By the end, you will know the three letters every company sends, what each one really promises, and the one line in the offer letter that decides whether it can be cancelled. Stay till the third letter because that is the one that actually protects you. Letter 1. The offer letter. This is the first letter you receive, usually after your final interview or campus drive. It says the company wants to hire you for a role at a salary. It feels like the finish line. But read it slowly and you will see words like subject to conditional and until verification. That is because an offer letter is usually an intention, not a final contract. The company is saying we would like to hire you if everything checks out. Things that commonly have to check out are your degree and final results, your documents, your background verification, and sometimes the company's own business needs. If any of these fail, the offer can be withdrawn. Letter 2. The joining letter. This one tells you where and when to report. It has a joining date, a location, a list of documents to carry, and often a portal link. Many freshers think the joining letter is the same as being hired for sure. It is a big step forward but it is still not the last step. Now here is the catch. Joining dates can move. Companies often onboard in batches and a batch can be pushed because of project needs. A move date is annoying but it does not mean your offer is cancelled. Do not panic and do not resign from anything else based on a date alone. What should you do when you get it? Save it, read every document on the list and confirm the instructions only from the official portal or the official email address. Letter 3. The appointment letter. This is the one that matters most and the one freshers forget to ask about. The appointment letter is the formal employment document. It usually comes on or after your joining once your documents are verified. It carries the real terms such as your designation, your pay structure, your probation, your notice period, and any service bond. So here is the rule. Treat the offer letter as a promise to try and the appointment letter as the real agreement. The terms in the two letters can differ. If your appointment letter shows a different pay structure or a new bond you never saw before, ask questions before you sign. Ask politely and ask in writing. Now the mistakes. And the third one is the one that gets people in real trouble. Mistake 1. Resigning from your current job or dropping other options the moment the offer letter arrives. If the offer is delayed or withdrawn, you are left with nothing. Keep your options alive until the appointment letter is in your hand. Mistake 2. Ignoring the small print. Look for words like conditional, subject to verification, and the right to withdraw. Know what you are agreeing to. Mistake 3. Giving wrong or incomplete information in your documents or forms. A small mismatch in a date, a name, or a mark. Can create a verification problem and a verification problem can cause you the offer. If something is wrong, tell the company early and with proof. Do not hide it. So what should you do this week? First, put all your letters in one folder on your phone and in an email draft to yourself. Second, read the offer letter once more and underline every condition. Third, check that your mark sheets, ID proofs, and certificates match each other exactly. Fourth, keep every email from the company and reply to requests quickly. And one safety point. A real company will never ask you to pay money for a joining letter or an appointment letter. If someone asks, stop and verify on the official website. So, to sum up. The offer letter is an intention, the joining letter is a schedule, and the appointment letter is the real agreement.
