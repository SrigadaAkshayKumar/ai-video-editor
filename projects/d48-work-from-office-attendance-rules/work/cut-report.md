## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your salary can be cut, and your first appraisal can be damaged without you doing a single thing wrong in your work just because of how your attendance looks in the system. If you are a fresher joining a tech company in the next five minutes, I will show you three attendance mistakes that hurt new joiners and the simple habit that protects you from all three. The third one is the one almost nobody checks. First, why does attendance matter at all? You are a fresher. You are hardly doing any work yet. Here is the reason. In most big companies, attendance is not a manager's opinion. It is a record. Your badge, swipe, your login to the attendance portal, your timesheet, and your leave balance are all linked together in one system. And that system feeds payroll and the HR team. So, a small mismatch does not stay small. It can turn into a salary deduction or a mail from HR or a remark in your file while you were still waiting to join your first project. Mistake number one. Assuming that the office rules you heard in your interview are the rules you will get. Many companies run a hybrid model where some days are in the office and some are at home. But the number of office days, which days, and which location you must report to can be set by your team, your client, or your project. And it can change after you join. So, do not rely on a friend's batch or a post on social media. Read your joining mail and the work mode section of your offer letter. Then ask your reporting manager one clear question in your first week. How many days, a week do I need to be in the office, and are they fixed days? Write the answer down. If it is only said on a call, send a short mail afterwards saying, as discussed, I will be in the office on these days. That one line protects you later. Mistake number two. Your swipe record and your attendance portal do not match. Here is a mini-story. Ravi reaches the office on time, but his badge is not activated yet. So, he enters through the visitor desk. He works the whole day. At the month end, the system shows him absent for those days. Nobody was blaming him. The record was simply empty. This happens a lot in the first weeks when badges, laptops, and portal access are still being set up. The fix is simple. If you worked, but did not swipe, or you worked from home on a day, the portal shows office, raise a regularisation request the same day, or the next morning. Tell your manager at the same time. Do not wait for the month end. Many companies close the attendance cycle on a fixed date. And after that, correcting it becomes slow and sometimes impossible. Now, mistake number three. The one almost nobody checks. Taking a day off in the wrong way. Think about this. You feel unwell, you tell a friend on your team, and you do not come. You did not apply for leave in the portal because you did not know how. A few days later, that day is marked as an unauthorised absence. In many companies, that can mean loss of pay for the day, and it can show up when the manager reviews your record. The same thing happens with late arrivals and early exits. Some companies count a fixed number of hours as a full day, and anything less as a half day. You may not see this until the pay is late. So, in your first week, do three things. Find out where leave is applied, find out the minimum hours that count as a full day, and always inform your manager by message before you apply, if it is an emergency. Message your first, apply right after. So, to sum up, attendance is a record, not an opinion. Know your office days and confirm them in writing, fix any swipe, mismatch the same day, and learn how leave and full days are counted before you need them.
