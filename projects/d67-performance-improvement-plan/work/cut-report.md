Your manager calls you into a room and hands you a paper. At the top, it says Performance Improvement Plan. Thirty days, and most freshers have no idea what to do next. If you are a fresher in your first year, or about to join, in the next five minutes, I will explain what a Performance Improvement Plan really is. The three mistakes that make it worse, and the one thing you should do on day one. Stay till the end, because the third mistake is the one that quietly ends careers. First, what is this thing? A Performance Improvement Plan, often called a PIP, is a formal document. Your employer gives it to you when they have concerns about your work. It sets clear targets that you must meet within a fixed period, usually 30 to 90 days. That is how Mint described it in October this year. Here is the part nobody tells you. It is not automatically a termination letter. On paper, it is a chance to fix the problem. In practice, some managers use it as a warning, and some companies use it before an exit. So you must take it seriously from the first day, but you must not panic. And one more thing, for a fresher, the plan usually comes after a few months of weak output, missed deadlines, or too many mistakes. That means you can see it coming, and you can avoid it mistake number one, saying yes and walking out without asking anything. When the plan arrives, many freshers nod, sign and go back to their desk. Then after three weeks, they find out they were measured on things they did not understand. Instead, ask for the plan in writing. Ask what exactly is expected, how it will be measured, and by which date, ask when the review meetings will happen. If the targets are vague, like improve your performance, ask for numbers. For example, how many tasks closed or how many bugs reopened. A real example of why. A Bengaluru employee posted online that he was placed on a plan in a meeting. But nobody gave him anything in writing. He was left confused and stressed. Do not be that person. Written targets protect you. Mistake number two. Hiding the real reason behind your poor work. Maybe you are struggling with the technology. Maybe you are sick or things are difficult at home. Many freshers say nothing because they are scared of looking weak. But your manager and HR can only help with what they know. Mint quoted a law firm saying employees can inform HR about any illness that is affecting their performance. And can send a written letter with their own views if they disagree with the plan. They can also ask for regular feedback. So write a calm short email. Thank your manager confirm the targets as you understood them and ask for a weekly check-in. Keep it polite. No anger. No blame. This email is your record. Now the third mistake. The one I promised. Sitting and waiting for the result. Some freshers do the work but they do not show the work or worse they start panicking and open job portals every night instead of fixing the problem. Here is the better way. Break each target into small weekly tasks. At the end of every week send your manager three lines. What I finished what is stuck and what I need. That is your proof. If the review comes you are not guessing you are showing. And do not let a plan make you stop asking for what is yours. Mint reported that poor performance alone does not make an employee ineligible for gratuity which the code on Social Security 20 protects except in certain cases. Rules on notice or severance depend on your contract so read your offer letter and appointment letter and ask HR politely. So to sum up a performance improvement plan is a formal document with a deadline. Do not stay silent. Do not hide the real reason and do not wait for the result. Get it in writing, send a calm email and report progress every week.
