## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

It is the first question in almost every interview, tell me about yourself. And most freshers lose the interviewer in the first 30 seconds. Because they answer it like this, my name is, my father is, my hobbies are. If you are a fresher preparing for placements or off-campus interviews, in the next five minutes I will give you a simple three-part answer and the two mistakes that quietly end good interviews. Stay till the end because the last part is the one that makes the interviewer ask better questions. First, understand what this question really is. The interviewer is not asking for your life story. They already have your resume, they are asking three silent questions. Can this person speak clearly? Does this person know what they are good at? And is this person right for this role? So, the question is not an introduction, it is your chance to choose the first topic of the interview. Whatever you say here, the interviewer will usually ask about next. That is why a good answer gives you control. Here is the catch. Most freshers spend this chance on things nobody can ask a follow-up question about. Your hobbies, your school, your family, you get a polite nod and then a random question from the resume. Here is the answer structure. Present, past, future. 30 seconds each, 90 seconds in total. Part one, present who you are right now in one line. For example, I'm a final year computer science student and I have been building web applications with Java and a database. Part two, past two proofs of that skill. One project, one result. For example, in my final year project I built a placement tracking app and I fixed a slow search by adding an index which made results come much faster. Pick things you can explain fully because the interviewer will pick one of them next. Part three, future. Why this role? For example, I want to start my career as a software developer and this role lets me work on real projects with a team. That is it, present, past, future. Now the two mistakes. The first is the memorized speech. Many students copy an answer from the internet and learn it by heart. The interviewer can hear it. The voice goes flat, the speed goes up and the moment they ask a follow-up question. You are lost. Ravi memorized a perfect paragraph about being a passionate team player. The interviewer asked which team Ravi had no answer. The speech did not help him because none of it was his own. So do not memorize sentences. Memorize only the three parts and the two proofs. Then say it in your own words differently every time. The second mistake is the long story. Some freshers talk for four or five minutes starting from 10th class. Remember, 90 seconds is the target. If your answer is longer than two minutes, you are not helping the interviewer. You are making them wait to ask what they want. Here is a simple test. Say your answer aloud while recording on your phone. If it is longer than two minutes, remove the school details first, then the hobbies and keep only what connects to the role. And one more thing, end with a clear stop. Do not tail off with and that is all sir. End with your future line and then stay quiet and smile. Now, the part I promised how to make the interviewer ask better questions. Leave a hook. In your past part, mention one thing you want them to ask about. For example, I fixed a slow search in my project. That single line invites the next question. How did you fix it? And now you are talking about something you know well. Compare the two. An answer full of hobbies leads to a random question. An answer with one clear hook leads to the question you prepared for. So, before any interview, write down your three parts. Choose your two proofs and pick the one hook. Practice it five times allowed, not in your head allowed. So, to sum up, tell me about yourself is not a question about your life. It is your chance to pick the first topic. Say present, past, future in 90 seconds. Do not memorize sentences. Do not tell a long story and leave one hook. Now, I want to hear from you guys.
