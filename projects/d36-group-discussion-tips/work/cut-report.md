## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Most freshers lose a group discussion in the first two minutes and not because they lacked ideas. This video is for every student preparing for campus or off-campus hiring rounds. By the end, you will know what panels actually watch. A simple way to start and the one mistake that gets loud people rejected. Stay till the third point because that is the one almost everybody gets wrong. First, let us fix a wrong idea. A group discussion is not a debate. Nobody is trying to win. The panel is usually watching four things. Can you communicate clearly? Do you listen to others? Can you work with a group and can you stay calm when people disagree? Your final opinion matters much less than these four. Think of Arjun. He knew a lot about the topic and he spoke for almost the whole time. He was not selected. Now, think of Meena. She spoke only three times but each time she connected toward the earlier person said and she brought the group back to the point she was selected. That is the difference between talking a lot and adding value. Point 1. Start with structure, not with opinion. The first person to speak often gets noticed. But only if the start is good, a weak start is worse than no start. So, use a simple opening in three short sentences. Say what the topic means, say why it matters today. Then say how you suggest the group should discuss it. For example, if the topic is work from home versus work from office, you could say, this topic is about where people do their best work. It matters because many companies are changing their rules. Let us look at both sides and then agree on what suits freshers. If you are not sure, do not rush. It is fine to speak second or third as long as your first point is clear. Point 2. Listen, then connect. While others speak, write one keyword from each point. When it is your turn, start with a link. Say I agree with what Meena said about training and I would add this or I see it slightly differently and here is why. This one habit shows the panel that you listen, that you are respectful and that you can build on ideas. It also protects you when you disagree because you are disagreeing with the idea never with the person. And if someone is not getting a chance to speak, invite them. A sentence like, we have not heard from you yet, what do you think makes you look like a leader? Now point 3. The one I promised, do not interrupt and do not fight. Many freshers think that speaking loudly and often shows confidence it does not cutting someone off. Raising your voice or arguing again and again shows that you cannot work in a team. And in a software company, you will work in a team from the first week. If someone interrupts you, stay calm. Say, let me finish this one point and then please go ahead. If the discussion turns into a shouting match, that is your chance. Say, we seem to have two views here. Can we list one point for each and decide? Panels remember the person who calms the room. Also avoid these small traps. Do not use your phone or notes unless allowed. Do not speak only in your mother tongue if the round is in English. And do not bring random statistics that you are not sure about because a wrong number hurts more than no number. So how do you practise this week? Pick one topic a day such as AI and jobs or online safety. Set a timer for 10 minutes with two or three friends. One person only watches and gives feedback on the four things clarity, listening, teamwork and calm. If you practise alone, record yourself for two minutes. Open with three sentences of structure. Make two points and close with a one-line summary. Then watch it once and fix one thing only. At the end of the real round, if the panel gives time, give a short summary of what the group agreed. It shows maturity and it is often the last thing they remember. To sum up, start with structure, listen and connect and never interrupt or fight.
