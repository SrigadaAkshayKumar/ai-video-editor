## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your first salary slip will show a tax cut and one form you fill in the first week decides how big that cut is. Most freshers never even see that form. This video is for every fresher who is about to join or just joined. By the end, you will know the one form to fill the tax rule that may put your tax at zero. And the mistake that makes your salary shrink for no reason. First, the basics. India has two ways to calculate your income tax, called the new regime and the old regime. The new regime is the default. That means, if you do nothing, your employer uses the new one. The new regime has lower rates and very few deductions. The old regime has higher rates, but you can claim things like house rent allowance or investments. For most freshers, on a first salary, the new regime is the simpler choice because you usually have few investments to claim. But the choice is yours and that is where the form comes in. Now, the number that surprises everyone. Under the new regime, there is no income tax at all if your taxable income is up to twelve lakh rupees. This comes from a rebate under section 87a of up to 60,000 rupees. Let me say that in simple words, taxable income is your salary after deductions. There is also a standard deduction of 75,000 rupees for salaried people. So, a fresher earning a few lakh a year will most likely pay zero tax at the end of the year. But here is the catch. Zero tax at the end of the year does not mean zero tax cut in your salary every month. Stay with me because this is the mistake I promised. Your employer deducts tax every month from your salary based on a guess of your whole year. This is called TDS. If your employer guesses wrongly or does not know your choice, too much tax can be cut. Then the extra money sits with the tax department until you file your return and claim a refund. That can take weeks or months. It is your money, but you cannot use it. So, here is how to avoid it. In the first week, tell your payroll team which regime you choose. Ask them to show a monthly tax estimate for the year. If your tax is expected to be zero and still a large amount is being cut, raise it with them early. Now the form. It is called Form 12BB in it. You give your employer details of anything you want counted for tax such as house rent if you choose the old regime. The income tax department describes it as the form an employee gives the employer so that the right tax is deducted. At the end of the financial year, your employer gives you another document, Form sixteen. It is your tax certificate. It shows your income deductions and the tax already deducted. Keep both safely. When you file your income tax return, Form sixteen is the paper you will need most. If you change your job later, give the new employer your old Form sixteen details or your tax can be counted twice. Let me recap. One, the new regime is the default. So, check what your employer is using. Two, up to twelve lakh of taxable income can mean zero tax. Three, in week one, tell payroll your choice and keep Form sixteen at the end of the year. Here is my question for you guys. Do you know which regime your employer is using for you?
