Your first salary has not even come yet, and the bank may already be counting your education loan interest. If you studied on a student loan, there is one date in your sanction letter that decides when your EMI starts. This video is for every engineering student and fresher with an education loan. In the next five minutes, I will explain the repayment holiday, the interest that keeps growing quietly, and a tax benefit that many freshers lose by choosing the wrong option. Stay till the end because the 4th check is the one people forget. First, what is this repayment holiday? Banks call it a moratorium. It means you do not pay any EMI while you study, and for some time after the course ends. As an example, the State Bank of India student loan page says repayment starts one year after the completion of the course, and the loan is then repaid over up to 15 years. So, you got a job, and you think, "I have not received any bank message, so nothing is due." That is the mistake. The date is not decided by your joining date. It is decided by your sanction letter. Different banks and different schemes can have different rules. So, the first check is simple. Open your sanction letter, find the worst course period plus repayment holiday, and write down the exact month your first EMI is due. Second check. The interest does not stop during the holiday. The same SBI page says the interest that builds up during the course period and the holiday period is added to the principal, and the EMI is then fixed on the new, bigger amount. Let me make it simple with an example. This is only an example, not your bank's number. Suppose 5 lakh rupees is outstanding at 10% a year. That is about 50,000 rupees of interest in one year. If you pay nothing for that year, the 50,000 is added to your loan, and you then pay interest on it as well. Now, the good news. The SBI page also says that if the full interest is paid before repayment begins, the EMI is fixed on the principal amount only. So, the second check is this. Ask your bank how much interest has built up till today and whether you can pay it even in small parts once your salary starts. Third check. The tax benefit, and here is the catch. Money control and outlook money both explain that the deduction for education loan interest applies to the interest part only, not the principal. It can be claimed for 8 years or until the interest is fully repaid, whichever comes first, and it is available under the old tax regime. Now, think about your first year. Many companies put you in the new tax regime by default where this deduction is not available. So, do not choose a regime blindly. Keep your interest certificate from the bank and compare both regimes before you submit your declaration to your company. Your tax rules can change every year. So, check the latest rule on the income tax portal before you decide. Fourth check. The one people forget, your bank must know where to reach you. Many freshers change their phone number, email and CT within 6 months of joining. If the bank message goes to an old number, you may miss your first EMI date. A missed EMI can mean extra charges and it can also hurt your credit score. So, here is what to do this week. 1. Find your sanction letter and note the first EMI month. 2. Update your mobile number and email with the bank. 3. Set a reminder one week before the first EMI date. 4. Ask the bank in writing for the exact amount and what happens if you pay the interest early. Let me recap. Know your date. Remember that interest grows. Compare your tax regime.
