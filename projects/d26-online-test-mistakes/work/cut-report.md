## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

You can be good enough to clear the online test and still get thrown out of it before anyone checks your score. This video is for every fresher with an online assessment coming and aptitude test, a coding round, or a company test link in your inbox. By the end, you will know the four mistakes that get candidates flagged or rejected and a simple plan for the last 24 hours before the test. First, understand what the test is doing. Most company tests do two jobs at once. One job is to measure your skill. The other job is to check that the person taking the test is really you, working alone with fair effort. That second job is why many tests use a webcam, screen monitoring, or a locked browser. Rules differ from company to company, so always read the instructions mailfully. But the pattern is common and the mistakes below break it again and again. And here is the catch. Most of these mistakes have nothing to do with how smart you are. Mistake number one. You open the test link only when the test starts. Then the camera does not work, the browser is the wrong one, the internet drops, or a software update begins in the middle. The timer keeps running and sometimes your attempt cannot be restarted. So do a system check the day before. If the company gives a practice link, use it, use a laptop, not a phone, unless the mail says otherwise. Plug in the charger, close every other app, and keep a mobile hotspot ready as a backup connection. Do these ones and you remove the most common reason for a test going wrong. Mistake number two. Switching tabs or leaving the test window, even for a second. Many platforms record every time the window loses focus. You may only be checking a word meaning, but the report just shows a tap switch. A few of these can raise a flag and a flagged attempt may not be looked at. Before you start, close chat, apps, mute notifications, and turn on do not disturb. If the rules say no, outside help, treat that as a hard rule. Do not keep a second device with answers beside you and do not let anyone sit next to you and whisper. Those are exactly the things the camera and the software look for. The third mistake is the one that quietly hurts honest students the most, so stay with me. Mistake number three. Spending too long on one question. A tough coding problem pulls you in and 40 minutes later you have one question, half done and no time left for the easy ones. Most tests give marks per question and partial solutions often score. So read all questions first and solve the easiest ones first, then come back for the hard ones. In coding rounds, first make a simple version that passes the sample cases, then improve it. A working simple answer beats a perfect answer you never finished. Mistake number four. Guessing blindly in aptitude. Some tests have negative marking and some do not. The instructions will say which. If wrong answers lose marks, skip the question you really cannot work out. If they do not, always answer. Now your plan for the last 24 hours. One, do a system check and read the instructions mail again. Two, practice one-time mock test. So the clock feels familiar. Three, sleep properly because a tired mind makes silly mistakes in easy questions. Four, sit in a quiet room with good light and keep your photo identity card nearby if the mail asks for it. During the test, breathe, read each question slowly and keep an eye on the timer. If something technical breaks, take a screenshot and mail the recruiter at once politely with the time and what happened. Do not stay silent and hope. So to sum up, check your system early. Never leave the test window. Do not get stuck on one question and know the marking rules before you guess.
