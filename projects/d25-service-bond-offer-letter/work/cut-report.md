## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Some offer letters have one small line that can cost you lakhs of rupees if you leave your first job too early, and most freshers sign it without reading. This video is for every fresher who has an offer letter or is able to sign one. By the end, you will know what a service bond is, what the Supreme Court has said about it, and the three questions to ask before you put your signature on it. Stay till the third question because that is the one that protects your money. First, what is a service bond? A service bond or minimum service agreement is a clause that says you must work for the company for a fixed period, usually one or two years. If you leave before that, you pay an agreed amount. Why do companies do this? Training of fresher costs, time, and money. The company wants to be sure you stay long enough for that effort to pay back. Many big service companies do not use a bond for regular fresher batches, but some smaller companies, product startups, and special training programs do. So, never assume. Read your own letter. Now, the big question is such a bond even legal. Here, the law has a rule, and it has a catch. Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act says an agreement that stops a person from doing a lawful profession is why. Many freshers think this kills every bond, but in May 2025, the Supreme Court looked at a case involving Vijaya Bank. An officer had agreed to serve three years or pay 2 lakh rupees. He left early and paid under protest, then went to court. The Supreme Court held that a minimum service clause is not a restraint of trade. The reason is simple. The clause does not stop you from working anywhere after you leave. It only says you must keep a promise you made during your employment, or pay the agreed amount. So, the answer is this. A reasonable bond can be enforced. Do not count on a court to cancel it for you. Now, the catch, the court did not say every bond is fine. In that case, the amount was not so high that leaving became impossible. The employee was also a senior manager with a good salary. For a fresher, think about it like this. Suppose your monthly salary is 20,000 rupees and the bond is 3 lakh rupees, that is 15 months of salary. The bond must be a fair estimate of the real cost to the company, not a punishment. An amount that looks like a penalty can be challenged. But a challenge means lawyers, time and stress. So, your real protection is not the court. It is reading the clause before you sign. So, here are the three questions. Question 1. How long is the bond and how much do I pay if I leave? Ask for both in writing, months and rupees, not vague words. Question 2. Does the amount reduce over time? A fair clause often reduces month by month. If you serve 18 months of a 24 month bond, you should not owe the full amount. Question 3. The one that protects your money. What exactly does the company spend on me? Ask if the bond is for paid training, a certification or relocation. If the company spends nothing on you, a heavy bond makes no sense. And one more thing, check the notice period and whether the company can hold your reliving letter. A bond dispute that ends with a held reliving letter is a common trap. What should you do this week? If you already have an offer, find the clause. Read it twice. Take a photo of the page and keep a copy of everything you sign. If something is unclear, ask the HR team by email so the answer is in writing. A polite email is not a risk, it is normal. If the amount is large and you are worried, talk to a lawyer before you sign, not after. And never pay any money to join a company because that is a different problem and usually a scam. You are allowed to ask questions. A good company will answer them. So, to sum up, a service bond can be legal. The Supreme Court has upheld a reasonable bond.
