## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

A joining bonus sounds like free money, but hidden in the same offer letter, there can be one line that makes you pay all of it back. If you are a fresher with an offer that mentions a joining bonus, a relocation bonus, or a retention bonus, this video is for you. By the end, you will know the four things to check in that clause, and the one mistake that makes freshers lose money even after they repay. First, what is a joining bonus? It is an extra amount a company offers to get you to say yes, and to actually join. Some companies call it a sign-on bonus. Some call it a relocation bonus if it is meant to cover your move to a new city. Here is the catch. Most of the time, this money is not a gift, it comes with a condition. The condition is that you stay with the company for a minimum period. If you leave before that period ends, you may have to return all or part of the money. That is called a clawback. And remember, this is not common for every fresher offer. Many campus offers have no joining bonus at all, but when it is there, the clause matters. Now, the four checks. Check number one, the stay period. How long must you stay? It may be 6 months, 12 months, or more. Find the exact words in your offer letter and find out when the clock starts. Does it start from your offer date or from your first day at work? Those are very different. Check number two, how much you repay. Is it the full bonus or only a part of it for the months you did not complete? Take an example. A company pays 50,000 rupees with a 12-month stay period. If you leave after 4 months, one clause may say you repay everything. Another clause may say you repay only the remaining 8 months, which is about 33,000 rupees. This is only an example, but it shows why the wording matters. Check number three, tax. A joining bonus is not tax-free. Under the Income Tax Act, salary includes fees, commissions, perquisites, and profits paid in addition to your salary. So, the bonus is taxed along with your salary, and the company deducts tax before or when it pays you. Here is the mistake I promised. Many freshers see the full bonus in the offer letter. Plan their money around it and forget that tax is cut from it. Then, if they leave early and repay the full amount, they have repaid more than they actually received in hand. If this happens to you, speak to a chartered accountant about how to handle the tax. Do not guess. Check number four, the paper trail. Does the company give the clause in writing, in the offer letter, or a separate agreement? Never agree to a bonus condition that is only spoken by a recruiter on a call. So, what should you do this week? Open your offer letter and search for the words bonus, recovery, repay, and retention. Read each line twice. If you find a clause you do not understand, ask the HR team to explain it by email. So, you have the answer in writing. Ask three simple questions. When does the stay period start? How is repayment calculated if I leave early? Is the bonus paid before or after I join? And if the clause is unclear, do not sign in a hurry because of the bonus. A bonus is useful only if you can keep it comfortably. Also, never pay any money to anyone to get a joining bonus. Real companies pay you, they do not ask you to pay first. So, to sum up, a joining bonus usually comes with a stay period. Check the period, check the repayment amount, remember the tax, and get everything in writing. Now, tell me in the comments.
