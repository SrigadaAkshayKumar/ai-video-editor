## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

A recruiter can look at your LinkedIn profile for 10 seconds and decide you are not worth a message, and most freshers lose their 10 seconds on the very first line. If you are a final year student or a fresher looking for your first tech job, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will show you the four parts of a profile that recruiters actually read, the three mistakes that make them scroll past, and a 10-minute fix you can do tonight. Stay till the end because the third mistake looks harmless, but it can cost you messages. First, why this matters? Before a recruiter calls you, they usually search. They type words like Java, Python, Fresher, or Testing into a search box, and then they open the profiles that come up. So, your profile is not a certificate, it is a search result. If the right words are not in it, you do not show up, and if you do show up, but the top of the page is empty, they leave. Think of two students from the same college with the same marks. One has a blank headline that says "Student at some college." The other has a headline that says "Final year BTEC, Python, and SQL built two projects." Only one of them looks like a person who is ready to work. Here are the four parts that get read. In order, part one, your photo. A clear face, plain background, good light, no sunglasses, no group photo. It does not need to be a studio shot. A phone photo near a window works. Part two, your headline. This is the line under your name, and it is the most important line on the page. Do not write only student. Write what you are, what you know, and what you want. For example, final year, computer science student, Java, and SQL looking for a software engineer role. Part three, your about section. Write three or four short lines. Who you are, which skills you have used, and what kind of role you want. Plain words, no big claims like hard-working team player with a passion for excellence. Every student writes that, so it tells the recruiter nothing. Part four, your projects. For each project, write one line on what it does. One line on the tools you used, and a link to the code if you have one. Now the three mistakes. Mistake number one, listing skills you cannot explain. If you write machine learning, cloud, and cyber security because they sound good, an interviewer will ask you about them, and you will be caught. Keep only the skills you can talk about for two minutes. Mistake number two, a profile that does not match your resume. If your resume says one college name, one year, or one project title, and your profile says another, it looks careless. Later, during background verification, the same details are checked against your documents, so keep them identical. Mistake number three, the harmless looking one, having no way to contact you. Many freshers turn off messages or leave an email address that looks like a joke. Use a simple email with your name in it, and make sure people can message you. Also keep your profile visible because a hidden profile cannot be found by a recruiter. Here is the 10 minute fix. Do it tonight. Step one, change your photo if it is not clear. Two minutes. Step two, rewrite your headline using the pattern I gave you. Roll, skills, and what you are looking for, two minutes. Step three, write three lines in about three minutes. Step four, add your two best projects with one line each, and remove any skill you cannot explain. Three minutes, then open your resume next to it, and check that the college name, year, and project titles are the same. That is the part most people skip. One more thing because freshers are the target here. A real recruiter will never ask you to pay money for a job, an interview slot, or a laptop. If someone on LinkedIn asks for a fee or tells you to move to a chat app and send your documents straight away, stop. Check the company on its official carriers page and see whether the recruiter is listed there. A real offer can always be verified. So, to sum up, your profile is a search result. So use the words "recruiter search for", fix your headline first, keep skills you can explain, match your resume, and stay reachable.
