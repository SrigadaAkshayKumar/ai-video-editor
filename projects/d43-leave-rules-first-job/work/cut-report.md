## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Taking leave in your first job can quietly hurt you, and nobody tells freshers the rules before day one. This video is for every fresher who is about to join or has just joined a tech company. By the end, you will know the three leave mistakes that cause trouble, and the exact message to send when you need a day off. Let me start with why this matters. In most companies, leave is not given all at once on the first day. It is usually credited month by month, so, in your first few months, you may have only a few days of leave in your account, or even none. And the rules are not the same everywhere. Every company has its own leave policy, and the state where your office is located also has its own rules for shops and establishments. So, never trust a friend's policy, your own offer letter, and the leave section of your company portal are the only sources that count. Here is the catch. If you take leave when your balance is empty, it is usually marked as loss of pay. That means a day of salary is cut for each such day. Mistake number one. Telling your manager on chat and thinking that is enough. Ravi was a new joiner. He told his team lead on a message that he would be away on Friday for a family function. The lead said, "Okay." But Ravi never applied in the leave system. At month end, his Friday showed as absent, and his salary was reduced. The rule is simple. A chat message is a courtesy. The leave application in the official system is the record. Do both every time. The second mistake is the one that surprises most freshers. Mistake number two. Taking leave during the training period or the probation period without asking how it works. In many companies, the first weeks are an induction or training programme, and attendance there can be strict. Missing a few days may mean you fall behind, or even have to repeat a module. Some companies also extend probation if attendance is low. Not all do, so you must ask. Here is what to do. In your first week, ask your trainer or manager one question. What is the leave rule during training and probation? Write the answer down. This single question can save you from a lot of stress. And now, mistake number three, the one that gets people noticed for the wrong reasons. Mistake number three. Going silent, maybe you are sick, or there is an emergency at home. It happens. But disappearing without a single message is what damages trust. A manager can handle a fresher who is sick. A manager cannot handle a fresher who is unreachable. So here is the message to send as early as you can in the morning. Say good morning, I am not well and will not be able to work today. I will apply for leave in the system and keep my phone with me for urgent work. Please let me know if anything needs my help. That is short, honest, and professional. Also for medical leave, keep your doctor's note. Some companies ask for it after a few days of absence. Now how do you plan leave smartly? First, check your balance before you plan anything. Second, apply early for planned days, especially around festivals and long weekends, because many people apply for the same days. Third, avoid taking leave right before a delivery date or a review in your project. And fourth, if you must be away at a bad time, tell your manager early and offer a plan, for example, who will cover your work. Remember, leave is your right under your company policy. The goal is not to avoid it. The goal is to take it in a way that keeps your manager on your side. So to sum up, check your own leave policy, apply in the system, and not only on chat. Ask about training and probation in week one and never go silent. Now tell me in the comments, have you ever been confused about a leave rule in your company or your offer letter?
