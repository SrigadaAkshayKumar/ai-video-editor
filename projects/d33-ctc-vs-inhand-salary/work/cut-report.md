Your offer letter says 12 lakh rupees. Your first salary will not be 1 lakh rupees, not even close, and nobody tells freshers why until the money lands in the bank. If you are about to join your first job in software, this video is for you. By the end, you will know the five things hiding inside your CTC. A simple way to estimate your real monthly salary before you sign, and the one question to ask the HR about wealth variable pay. Stay for the fourth one, because it is the part that surprises almost everyone. First, what is CTC? It stands for Cost to Company. It is the total amount the company spends on you in one year. It is not the amount that comes to your bank account. Think of it like a restaurant bill. The total on the bill includes the food, but also tax and service charge. Your CTC includes your salary, but also money the company puts aside for you, and money that you only get if certain conditions are met. So, when the offer says 12 lakh, the company is saying this is what you cost us. Your job is to find out how much of it is yours every month. Here are the five parts that sit inside your CTC, but never reach your account every month. One. The company's share of provident fund. The company puts money into your PF account for you. It is yours, but you get it later. Two. Grativity. This is a retirement type benefit that the company counts in your package. It is paid when you leave after the minimum service period. So, do not plan your rent around it. Three. Insurance and other benefits. Health cover, meal cards, and similar things are counted at their cost to the company. Four. And this is the one that surprises people. Variable pay. Suppose a package is 15 lakh, and 2 lakh of it is performance linked. That 2 lakh may be paid yearly or quarterly, and the amount can change with your rating or the company's targets. It is not guaranteed money. Five. Joining or retention bonuses. These can be inside the package with conditions like staying for a year. Now, let me show you the maths with an example. This is only an illustration, and your company's numbers will differ. Say your CTC is 12 lakh a year. Dividing by 12 gives 1 lakh a month. But after taking out the company's PF share, gratuity, bonus, and insurance, your monthly gross salary may come to around 85 to 90 thousand rupees. From that gross salary, your own PF share, income tax, if it applies, and other small deductions are cut. If they come to 8 to 10 thousand rupees a month, your take home lands at roughly 75 to 82 thousand rupees, so a 1 lakh a month dream becomes about 75 to 82 thousand in reality. The gap is not achieved. Part of it is saved for you in PF, part of it is tax, and part of it is simply a benefit that you cannot spend. There is one more thing that is new. The new labor code says that wages, which means basic pay plus a few allowances, must be at least half of your total pay. PF and gratuity are worked out on those wages. What does this mean for you? Reports say companies are restructuring salary parts to fit this rule. A higher wage part means more going to PF and gratuity, which is more long-term saving, but it can also mean a slightly lower monthly take home. So do not compare two offers by CTC alone. Compare the monthly enhanced salary and ask how the package is split. Here is what to do before you sign or join. Step 1. Ask the HR for the full salary breakup sheet, not only the CTC number. Every company has one. Step 2. Find the monthly gross and the monthly deductions. Subtract them to estimate your take home. Step 3. Ask this exact question, how much of my CTC is fixed and how much is variable and when is the variable paid? Step 4. Ask if there are any joining bonus conditions and what happens if you leave early. Do these four steps and you will never be shocked by your first salary. To sum up, CTC is what you cost the company, not what you earn each month. Five parts hide inside it, variable pay is never guaranteed and the only number that pays your rent is the monthly enhanced salary.
