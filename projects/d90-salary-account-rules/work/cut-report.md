## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your first salary can land in an account you did not choose, and your own bank account may never see a rupee of it. If you are about to join a tech company, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, you will learn what a company salary account is, the three rules that decide if it stays free, and the one mistake that quietly costs freshers money after they change jobs. The third rule is the one almost nobody checks. First, the basics on your joining day, the human resources team will often give you a bank form. The company has a tie-up with one bank, and that bank opens a salary account for you, usually with no minimum balance. Your salary will be credited there every month. Why does the company do this? One bank file is easier to process than thousands of different accounts. It is not a trick, it is how payroll works in most large companies. But here is the catch, that free zero-balance status does not belong to you, it belongs to the link between your company and the bank. Keep that line in mind, because it explains everything that comes next. Rule number one, know what you actually get. Salary accounts usually come with a free debit card, a higher daily withdrawal limit, and sometimes extra benefits such as insurance cover or cheaper loans. These differ from bank to bank and from company to company. So do not trust a friend's account. Ask the bank for the printed benefit sheet for your company's plan. Why does this matter? Because you may be offered a loan or a credit card by the same bank in your first month. Check the interest rate and charges before you say yes. A friendly bank officer is still a salesperson. Rule number two, this is the rule that surprises people. If your salary stops coming into the account, the bank will usually convert it into an ordinary savings account. Reports from financial news outlets say this typically happens after about three months without a salary credit, though the exact period depends on the bank. After the conversion, your card still works, your UPI still works, nothing looks different. But now a minimum balance rule can apply and if your balance falls below it, the bank can charge a penalty. A few such charges can hit a small balance without any warning. Here is a mini story. Ravi changed companies after 14 months and forgot about his old salary account. It had a few hundred rupees. Months later, the penalties had pulled it down to almost nothing. Nobody wanted him because nobody checked. Now, rule number three, the one almost nobody checks. Before you leave your first company, plan your old account. Step one, check your balance and the minimum balance rule on that bank's website or ask at the branch. Step two, tell your new company's human resources team which account to use. Some new companies will ask you to open an account with their bank. Ask if you may give your existing account instead. Many companies allow a request like this, but it depends on the company. Step three, move every automatic payment, your EMI, your SIP, your subscriptions, your insurance. If any of these is still linked to the old account and the balance is low, a payment can fail. A failed EMI can hurt your credit score. Step four, do not just abandon the account. Either keep a small balance above the minimum or close it properly and ask for a closure confirmation. So, what should you do this week even before you join? Keep one personal savings account that is yours and never depends on any company. Use it for your important payments like your loan EMI or your SIP. Then when a salary account closes or converts, your life does not break. Also, never share your card number, your OTP or your net banking password with anyone even if they say they are from the company or the bank. Real banks do not ask for them. And one more thing, keep your bank statements. You may need them for a loan, a visa or your tax filing later. Let me recap. Your salary account is free because of your company's link with the bank. If the salary stops, it can turn into a normal savings account and before you switch jobs, move every payment and plan the old account.
