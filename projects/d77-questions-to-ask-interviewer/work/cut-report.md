## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

The interview is almost over. You feel relieved. And then the interviewer says, "Do you have any questions for us?" And most freshers say, "No, sir. Everything is clear." That one sentence quietly loses points. If you are a fresher giving interviews, in the next five minutes, I will show you why this last question matters. The three questions that make you look serious, and the two questions you should never ask, stay till the end because the second one is the question that interviewers remember. First, why do interviewers ask this? It is not a formality. By this point, they already know what you studied. Now, they want to know how you think. Are you curious? Did you listen? Do you care about the work, or only about the offer? Think about it from their side. Two freshers have the same marks and the same answers. One says, "No questions." The other asks a sharp question about the team. Who sounds more ready for a job? The good news is that you do not need to be clever. You only need to prepare. Write three questions before the interview, keep them in your notebook, and pick the ones that fit the conversation. Question one. What does a fresher in this role work on in the first three to six months? This shows that you are thinking about doing the job, not just getting it. And the answer gives you real information. Will you get training first? Will you be placed on a project? Will you work with a mentor? You can say it like this, "Sir, I would like to understand what a typical first six months looks like for someone in this role. What would you expect me to learn?" Notice the last part, you are not asking what the company will give you. You are asking what you should learn. That small change in wording makes a big difference. Question two. The one interviewers remember, "What does a successful fresher in this team do differently?" Most candidates never ask this, and it is powerful because it makes the interviewer describe their ideal fresher. They will say things like, asks questions early, finish your tasks on time, learns the tools quickly." Now you know exactly what they want. If you get the job, you already have a target, and if the interview is still going on, you can say, "That is something I have done in my final year project," and give a short example. So this one question gives you information and a chance to add one more point in your favour. That is a very good deal for ten seconds of talking. Question three. What are the next steps, and when can I expect to hear back? This sounds simple, but it solves a real problem. Many freshers wait for weeks, not knowing if there is another round or if they were rejected. Some companies reply in a few days, others take longer, and some only contact selected candidates. When you ask, you get a clear picture, and you also look organised. Then, if the time they gave passes, you can send a polite follow-up message, and you will not feel like you are disturbing them. Write down what they say. If they say, "Within one week, note the date." That is your follow-up date. Now the two questions you should never ask in this round. First, never ask about salary, leave or work from home as your opening question. Those are fair topics, but they belong to the offer stage. If they are your only questions, the interviewer thinks you care only about benefits. Second, never ask something you could find in thirty seconds on the company website, like what does your company do? That tells them you did not prepare, and one more small trap. Do not ask, "Did I do well?" It puts the interviewer in an awkward position, and their answer will not help you. Ask about the role or the team instead. So, to sum up, when the interviewer asks, if you have questions, never say no. Ask what the first six months look like, ask what a successful fresher does differently, and ask about the next steps. Keep salary and leave for the offer stage, and keep your questions about the role.
