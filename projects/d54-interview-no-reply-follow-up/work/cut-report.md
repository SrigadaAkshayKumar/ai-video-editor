## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

You finished your interview, the recruiter said, "We will get back to you," and now it is silence. No mail, no call, nothing. And the worst thing you can do now is the thing most freshers do. If you are a fresher or a final year student waiting for a reply after an interview, this video is for you. In the next five minutes, I will tell you how long you should wait the exact follow-up mail to send, and the three mistakes that quietly kill your chance. Stay till the end, because the third mistake is the one that feels polite but makes recruiters ignore you. First, why does the silence happen? Most of the time, it is not a rejection. The recruiter is usually handling many roles at once. The hiring manager may be on leave, feedback from the interview panel may be pending, or the company is still interviewing other candidates and decisions only come when the whole batch is finished. A rejection can also be silent. Some companies simply do not send a mail to every candidate. So, the silence means one of two things, still waiting or no. And the only way to know is to follow up properly. The good news is that a polite follow-up is normal. Recruiters expect it and it shows you are interested. Now, when should you follow up? Here is a simple rule. Look at what the recruiter told you. If they said you will hear in one week, wait for that week to finish and then send one mail the next working day. If nobody gave you any time, wait about five working days. Before that, send only one message and that is a thank you note on the same day or the next morning of your interview. Keep it to three lines. Thank them for their time, say one thing you like about the role and say you look forward to the next step. Do not message every day, do not message the recruiter and the interviewer and the company page at the same time. One clear message to one person is enough. Here is the mail that works. Keep it short so the recruiter can read it in 10 seconds. The subject line following up, your role name, interview on the date and your name then three short lines. Line one, hello and the recruiter's name. Line two, I attended the interview for this role on that date and I am still very interested. Line three, could you please share any update on the next steps and then thank you, your full name, your phone number. Notice what is not in the mail, no complaints, no why have you not replied, no long story about your life. Just a clear question that is easy to answer and if the recruiter's mail is not in your inbox, use the same mail thread of your interview invite that way. They see the full history in one click. Now the three mistakes. Mistake number one, following up too early or too many times, if you send a mail after one day and again after two days, the recruiter sees pressure, not interest. One mail after the promised time and a second one only after another week. After that, move on. Mistake number two, showing anger or desperation. Lines like, I have another offer, reply today or please, I really need this job, make a recruiter uncomfortable. If you really do have another offer with a deadline, say it in one calm, honest line and ask if a decision is possible by that date. Never invent an offer. Recruiters talk to each other and a lie is easy to check. Mistake number three, the one that feels polite, waiting for this one company and stopping everything else. Recruiters stop applying, stop practicing and just refresh their inbox for three weeks. Then the answer is no and they have lost a whole month. The rule is simple. After you send the follow up, go back to your next application and your next practice round. Treat every reply as a bonus, not a rescue. So what do you do right now? Open your mail and find the interview invite. Write down the date the recruiter promised. If it has passed, send the three line follow up we just saw. Then open your application list and apply to two more roles today. To sum up, silence is usually not a no. Follow up once, politely at the right time and keep going whatever the answer is.
