## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Your first pull request gets 23 comments, and your first thought is, they think I am useless. If you are a fresher in your first year, or about to join in the next five minutes, I will explain what a code review really is, the three rules to handle every comment, and the one reply that quietly damages your name. Stay till the end, because the third rule is the one senior engineers notice first. First, what is a code review when you finish a piece of work? You do not put it straight into the main code. You raise a pull request, your teammates read your changes and leave comments, only after they approve does your code go in. Here is the part nobody tells freshers, reviewers are not grading you, they are protecting the product because after your code goes in, the whole team has to live with it. Even senior engineers get comments on almost every pull request, so a long list of comments does not mean you failed, it means people took the time to read your work. Rule one, read every comment before you reply to any of them. When you see the first comment, your mind wants to defend. You type a quick answer, and then the next comment explains why that answer was wrong. Now you have a messy thread. Instead read all the comments once, slowly, sort them in your head into three groups, things you must fix like a bug or a missing check. Things that are style, like names and spacing. And things you do not understand, this takes five minutes, but it turns 23 scary comments into maybe six real tasks. Rule two, if you do not understand a comment, ask, do not argue, and do not guess. Suppose the reviewer writes, please handle the null case. You do not know what they mean. Do not stay silent and change random lines. Reply like this, thanks for catching this. Do you mean when the user list is empty or when the value itself is missing? A question like that shows you are thinking, not defending. And if you truly disagree, give a reason and not an opinion, I used this approach because of this case, happy to change it if you prefer. Most reviewers respect that. Now the third rule is coming, and it is the one that decides how they see you. Rule three, fix it, close the loop and never make the same mistake twice. Fix the comments, push your changes and reply to each one with a short word, done, or fixed in the latest commit that way the reviewer does not need to hunt through your code again. Then comes the part that builds your name. Keep a small notes file, every time a reviewer teaches you something, write one line, use meaningful names, add a test, check for empty values. By your third pull request, those same comments should stop coming. That is the moment senior engineers start trusting you with bigger work. Now the reply that quietly damages your name, it works on my machine, or this was already like that, or worst of all saying nothing and merging your own code to avoid more comments. Reviewers do not mind a bug. Bugs happen, what they remember is a fresher who argues, ignores comments, or hides things. If you made a mistake, say it in one line, you are right, I missed this, fixing it now. That sentence costs nothing, and it makes people want to help you. So to sum up, comments are not about you, they are about the code, read everything first, ask when you are unclear, and fix and reply to every comment. Keep notes and you will grow faster than most of your batch. Now I want to hear from you guys. Comment the toughest review, comment you have ever got, and I will help you word the reply.
