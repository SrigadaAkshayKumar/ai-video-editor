"The interview went well, then the interviewer smiles and says, 'Do you have any questions for us?' And you say, 'No, sir, everything is clear.' That one sentence has quietly cost many freshers their offer. If you are preparing for interviews in the next five minutes, I will explain why they ask this. The two answers that hurt you, and three questions that make you look like someone worth hiring. Stay till the end, because the third question also tells you whether this is a good company to join. First, why do they ask this at all? It is not a polite ending. It is the last test of the interview. The interviewer wants to see two things. Are you curious about the work and do you think before you speak? Remember, the interviewer has talked to many candidates that day. Most of them say, 'No questions.' A candidate who asks one sharp question stands out because it shows you are already imagining yourself doing the job. So, the rule is simple. Never say no questions. Walk in with questions ready. The same way you walk in with your resume. Now, the two answers that hurt you. Mistake number one is asking about salary. Leave or work from home as your very first question. These are fair things to want to know. But if they are the only things you ask, the interviewer hears this person cares about the benefits, not the work. Imagine a ten minute interview where the only question you ask is, 'How many holidays do I get?' Save those questions for the H.R. round or the offer stage. In the technical round, ask about the work. In the H.R. round, you can ask about the process and once you have an offer, you can ask about salary structure and benefits openly. Mistake number two is asking something you could have found in two minutes on the company website. What does your company do? How many employees do you have? Which cities are your offices in? When you ask this, the interviewer hears one thing I did not prepare. Here is the fix. Before every interview, spend fifteen minutes on the company page and the job description. Note what they build, who their clients are and which tools the job post mentions. Then ask a question that builds on what you read. For example, I saw that your team works on cloud migration. What does a new joiner usually start with there? That question shows homework and interest in the same sentence. Now the three questions to carry into the room. Question one, what would a successful first ninety days look like for someone in this role? This tells the interviewer you already think about results and it tells you what the job really expects. Question two, what does a new joiner usually work on first and who would guide me? This shows you are ready to learn and it quietly tells you whether there is real training and support. Question three, the one I promised. What do you enjoy most about working on this team? Interviewers love this one because it makes them talk about themselves. And listen carefully to the answer. A long, warm answer is a good sign. A short, awkward pause is information too. You only need to ask one or two of these. Pick the ones that fit the conversation and ask them in your own words. So what do you do this week? Step one, write three questions in your notebook for every interview, not just one because the interviewer may answer one of them during the talk. Step two, Practise saying them out loud once. They should sound like curiosity, not like reading from a list. Step three, when the interviewer answers, do not jump to the next question. Say that is helpful and add one small follow up. A real conversation beats a perfect list. And if you really cannot think of anything, say this. I have understood the role well from our discussion. Could you tell me what the next steps are and when I can expect to hear from you? That is a safe, polite closing question and it is much better than silence. So to sum up, never say no questions. Do not open with salary and leave. Do not ask what the website already tells you. Ask about the first ninety days, the first project and the team. Now I want to hear from you. What is the question you are most scared of in an interview or the one you always forget to ask? Comment it below and I will read the best ones.
