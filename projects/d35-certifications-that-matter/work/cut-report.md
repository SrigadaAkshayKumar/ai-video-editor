## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

Before you pay for any certificate, hear this. Most fresh- freshers spend money on the wrong ones, and the right one costs less than a phone cover. This video is for every student and fresher who is waiting to join, or already on the bench, and wondering which certification is actually worth it. By the end, you will know the three questions that decide it, the one type of certificate that is worth starting with, and the trap that wastes thousands of rupees. First, the hard truth, a certificate does not get you a job by itself. A recruiter spends a few seconds on your resume, a certificate helps only when it proves a skill that the job really needs. Think of Ravi, he collected eight certificates in six months, all from short online courses, with no project and no exam. In his interview, the panel asked one practical question, and he could not answer. The eight certificates did not help. Now, think of Sneha, she took one proper exam, built one small project with it, and could explain both. That is the difference, so here are the three questions. Question one, is there a real proctored exam? A certificate that you get just by watching videos and clicking next proves very little. A certificate from a timed exam taken at a test centre or with an online proctor proves much more. Employers know the difference. For example, the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner is a foundational exam. According to Amazon's own certification page, it is ninety minutes long, has 65 questions, and costs 100 US dollars. It is meant for people with no earlier experience in cloud. Microsoft's Azure Fundamentals is also a beginner level certification, and it is a common starting point for a career in Azure. Question two, does it match the work? You will actually do. If your company puts you on a cloud project, a cloud basics certificate is useful. If your work is testing, learn a testing skill. If it is data, learn databases and data tools. Do not pick a certificate because it is trending. Ask your trainer, your senior, or look at the skills listed in the job posts for the role you want. And here is the catch most people miss. Your company may already pay for training or even for exams. Many large companies have their own learning platforms for employees. So before you pay from your own pocket, ask your manager or your training team if the exam fee is covered. Question three, can you show it in practice? After the exam, do one small thing with it. If you studied cloud, host a simple page and write down the steps. If you studied databases, build a small project and explain every table. Then one line on your resume can say what you built, not only what you passed. Here is a simple plan. Week one and two, learn the basics. Week three, take the practice questions. Week four, book the exam. Then spend one week on a small project. That is about five weeks. And you can do it while waiting to join or on the bench. Now the trap I promised, beware of anyone who says a certificate guarantees a job or asks you to pay a big fee for a package of certificates. A guarantee about a job is a warning sign. Before you pay anyone, check the price on the official exam page. If the official price is a fraction of what they are asking, you know the answer. Also, never share your exam login or personal details with a stranger who promises to pass the exam for you. That can get you banned and it can end a career before it starts. So to sum up, ask three questions. Is there a real exam? Does it match your work? Can you show it in a project? One good certificate with one project beats eight without.
