## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

You can know every answer in the technical round, and still lose the job in the last 10 minutes. In the HR round, this video is for every fresher who has an interview coming, on campus or off-campus. By the end, you will know the three answers that quietly get freshers rejected, what to say instead, and one simple method to answer any HR question in under a minute. First, why does this round exist? By now, the technical panel has already decided that you can do the work. The HR person is asking a different question. Will this person stay, learn, and be easy to work with? That means the HR round is not a formality. It is a risk check. Every answer you give is either lowering the risk or raising it. And here is the catch. Most freshers prepare perfect sentences from the internet, and the interviewer has heard those exact sentences a hundred times. So, the first rule is simple. Be specific and be true. Wrong answer, number one. Tell me about yourself and you read your resume aloud. Name, college, branch, marks, hobbies. The interviewer already has that paper in front of them. You have used your best minute to say nothing new. Instead, use three parts. Who you are in one line. One thing you built or learned that you are proud of. And what kind of work you want to do next. That is it. 30 to 40 seconds and then stop. For example, I am a final year computer science student. I built a small attendance app for my class and learned how to fix bugs after real users complained. Now I want to start as a developer and grow into back-end work. Wrong answer, number two. The moment the interviewer asks about expectations, you push for a big number or you say whatever the company gives. Both are risky. A very high number makes you look unaware of fresher pay. And saying anything is fine makes you look like you have not thought about your own value. A safer answer is this. I have read that freshers in this role are paid as per the company's standard fresher range and I'm open to that. My focus right now is learning and a good project. Short, calm and it shows you did your homework. But here is the part most people get wrong and it is the next mistake. So stay with me. Wrong answer, number three. And this is the one that gets the most freshers rejected. Why should we hire you? And you say I am hardworking, a quick learner and a team player. Every candidate says these words so they carry zero weight. Words like these are claims. The interviewer wants proof. Use the proof method. Pick one quality then give a small real story. For example, instead of saying I'm a quick learner, say in my third year I had never used databases and in two weeks I built a project with one. And I can show it to you. One story is worth more than 10 adjectives. And if you have no project yet, use a lab, a college event or even a course you finished. It just has to be real. Now the simple method that works for almost any question. Answer first, prove second, link to the job. Third, one sentence each. This week do three things. First, write your own answers to these three questions in your own words and say them aloud. Second, record yourself on your phone once and watch it. Third, ask a friend to interrupt you with a surprise question because real interviews never follow your script. If you can do this, you will sound calm even when the question is new. So to sum up, do not read your resume, do not shout a number or say anything is fine. And never rely on adjectives. Show one real story.
