Cognizant says it plans to hire up to 25,000 freshers this year. Every big tech company says it is hiring. So, why are lakhs of freshers sitting at home with letters of intent, timeline mails and no joining date? Both things are true at the same time, and once you understand why you will make much better decisions about your own career. In this video, I will give you the four real reasons tech companies are bringing in fewer freshers in 2026. And the fourth reason decides what you should do next. First, let's understand the contradiction. Hiring and onboarding are two different things. Companies still run big campus drives and they still select large numbers of students. That is the hiring part. But bringing those students into the company, giving them a joining date and a desk, that is onboarding, and onboarding is where the slowdown is. For example, Cognizant reported that out of about 20,000 graduates, it hired in 2025, around 16,000 were already working on client projects. So, freshers are being used. But at the same time, its total headcount actually fell slightly by about 900 people between March and June this year. So companies are selecting, but they are very careful about when they let people in. Here is why. Reason 1. AI is doing part of the routine work that used to go to freshers. Think about what a fresher traditionally did in the first year. Basic testing, writing simple code, fixing small bugs, writing documentation, and handling support tickets. Today, AI coding assistants and automation tools can do a good part of that work faster. This does not mean AI replaces every fresher. But it means one experienced engineer with AI tools can now do what used to need a small team. So for the same project, the company needs fewer new joiners. Reason two. Clients are cautious. Indian tech companies earn most of their money from clients in America and Europe. When those clients feel uncertain about the economy or about global events, like the tensions in West Asia, they delay new projects and cut non-essential spending. No new project means no new team. And no new team means no new seats for freshers. That is why many companies say they will onboard you based on business requirements. It literally means when a client project needs you. Reason three. The bench. In tech companies, the bench means employees who are already on the payroll but not working on any client project. When projects are slow, the bench grows. Now put yourself in the company's place. If you already have trained people sitting on the bench, you will move them to a new project first before onboarding a brand new fresher. So a big bench quietly pushes fresher joining dates further away. And Reason four. The one that matters most for your career. The shift from generalists to specialists. Companies are not just hiring fewer people. They are hiring different people. They are still recruiting actively for AI, data engineering, cloud, cyber security, and DevOps skills. TCS is reportedly building a large team of AI engineers who work directly with clients. Infosys plans to move between a quarter and a third of its workforce into specialist roles. Cognizant is building a group of what it calls Frontier Certified Engineers. So the demand has not disappeared. It has moved. The general fresher who knows a little of everything is waiting longer. The fresher with one clear in-demand skill and a project to prove it is the one getting picked. So what should you do with this? One pick one specialist direction, not five. Data, cloud AI, or cyber security. Choose the one that interests you and go deep. Two, build one real project in that area and put it on GitHub. A project is proof a certificate alone is not three. Keep your options open. If you are waiting on one company, keep applying to others. Midsize companies and product companies are hiring too. And four, if you get in through the regular fresher route, use your training and your first project to move towards that specialist skill. Internal moves are much easier than starting from outside. To sum up, companies are still selecting freshers, but onboarding is slow because of AI cautious clients and a large bench. And the demand is shifting towards specialists.
