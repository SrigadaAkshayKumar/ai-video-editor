## Clean script (what the viewer will hear)

You have two offer letters in your inbox. One is from the company you already accepted. The other just arrived and the salary is better. What do you do? If you are a fresher or a final year student, in the next five minutes I will explain the three mistakes that quietly cost people both offers and the exact steps to handle a second offer safely. Stay till the end because the third mistake is the one company's remember for years. Hiring is slow and uncertain. So many students sit for several companies. One offer comes in November, another arrives in February, nobody did anything wrong. Having two offers is a good problem. But here is the catch, the moment you accept an offer, you have usually signed or clicked an acceptance. Many offer letters say the offer is conditional on your joining by a date and that the company can withdraw it and in some cases you may be asked to compensate for notice or training. Companies differ, so the first thing to do is read your own letter line by line. Remember one rule, you can decide to leave a company before joining it. But how you leave decides whether the door stays open. Mistake number one, going silent. Some students simply stop replying to the first company and never turn up. This is called ghosting and it is the fastest way to be remembered badly. Recruiters talk to each other. Many large companies also share candidate records with their staffing partners and background checkvendors. A fresher who disappears can be marked as not interested or worse as a no show. Instead send a short honest email, thank them say you have received another opportunity that fits your plans and that you are withdrawing your acceptance. 10 lines is enough, do it as soon as you have decided not on the joining day. Mistake number two, accepting the new offer before you check it. A better salary on a page means nothing if the offer is not real or not final. Check three things, is the mail from the company's official domain and does the career's page or the recruiter confirm it? Does the letter clearly state your role, location, joining date and the salary breakup and is there any fee asked for anything at all? A genuine company never asks you to pay for a job. Also compare the offers on the right numbers, fixed pay, not the big CTC figure. Bonuses that are variable are not guaranteed and look at the location, the bond and the training period. A higher number with a longer bond is not always a better offer. I have a full video on spotting fake offers and another on CTC versus in-hand salary if you want more detail. Mistake number three, the one company's remember, leaving a mess behind. That means telling the first company at the last minute, ignoring their calls or not returning laptops, documents or training material if you have already received them. It also means telling a friend inside the company before you tell the company. Here is the catch, your first employer may be a recruiter's client, a partner or a company you want to apply to again in two years. A clean exit costs you one polite email. A messy exit can follow you through background verification because some employers check how you left earlier commitments. So be early, be polite, return everything and keep a copy of every mail you send. So here is the safe order. Step one, read your first offer letter for the joining date, withdrawal terms and any bond. Step two, verify the second offer from official sources. Step three, compare fixed pay, bond, location and role, not just the headline number. Step four, decide and only then write to the company you are leaving. Step five, accept the new offer in writing and keep every mail. And if your first offer has a serious penalty clause, talk to the recruiter about it before you decide. Do not guess what it means. Ask them to confirm it in writing. One more thought, a second offer is also a chance to negotiate honestly with the company you prefer but never invent an offer you do not have that can end your candidacy immediately. To sum up, having two offers is good news. Do not ghost, do not accept before you verify and do not leave a mess. Read your letter, check the second offer and write one honest email.
